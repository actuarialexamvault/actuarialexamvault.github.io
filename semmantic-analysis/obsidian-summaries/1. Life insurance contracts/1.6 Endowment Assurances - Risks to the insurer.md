
> [!trending-down] Investment Risk
> 
> The savings nature of the contract exposes the insure to investment risk, the extend of which depends on the structure.
> - ***Without profits***: the insurer bears most risk because benefits are fully guaranteed.
> - ***With-profits***: the risk is lower because the company can mitigate poor investment performance by reducing future bonus declarations, though they must still meet initial guarantees and manage policyholder's reasonable expectations.
> - ***Unit-linked***: the policyholder bears most of the risk, but the insurer faces a secondary risk. If fund values fall, the insurer's fund management charges will decrease.

> [!skull] Mortality Risk & Longevity Risk
> The risk that more deaths occur than expected. The nature of the death benefit determines the level of mortality risk:
> - ***High initial mortality risk***: if the contract pays a significant benefit on death (e.g. equal to the survival benefit), the sum at risk (the benefit minus the reserve) is highest at the start of the policy.
> - ***[[Anti-selection]] risk***: the risk that individuals in poor health will seek insurance to obtain favourable terms, which insurers can mitigate through medical underwriting.
> - ***Longevity risk***: for pure endowments, the insurer losses money if fewer policyholders die than expected, a risk that increases as the policyholder approaches maturity.

> [!receipt] Expense Risk
> There is an expense risk in that the actual [[Marginal costs|marginal costs]] of administering the contract exceed the loadings built into the premiums. 
> - ***Inflation***: Higher than expensed price inflation or earnings inflation can drive up administrative costs over longer terms.
> - ***Low sales volumes***: Since many costs are fixed, selling fewer policies than expected means each policy must carry a higher share of overheads to remain profitable.

> [!lapse] Withdrawal (Persistency) Risk
> The risk that the number of policyholders who surrender or lapse their policies differs from expectations.
> - ***Negative asset share***: If a policyholder withdraws when the asset share is negative (in the early years of regular premium policies due to high initial commission and setup costs), the insurer suffers direct financial loss.
> - ***Selective withdrawal***: Healthy policyholders are more likely to withdraw, potentially leaving the insurer with a sub-standard pool of lives who are more likely to claim death benefits earlier than expected. 

> [!group] Group Risks
> The [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/1.5 Endowment Assurances - Group version|group version]] of the contract adds no addition risks and anti-selection risk is much reduced if its compulsory for all members to join the group contract. 
> - ***[[Concentration risk]]***: A single event, such as an industrial accident, could result in a large number of simultaneous claims.

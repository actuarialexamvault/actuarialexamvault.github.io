
> [!skull] [[Mortality Risk]]
> - The significance of mortality risk depends heavily on the age of the policyholder and the duration the policy has been in force.
> - For **newly issued policies on** **older lives**, mortality is high because the sum assured is very large relative to the small reserve
> - For policies that have been in force for a long time, the mortality is relatively low because the insurer has already built up a larger reserve to cover the eventual claim.
> 
> A mortality risk may also arise from **[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Selective withdrawal]]**
> - The policyholders most likely to withdraw from the contract are those in good health, leaving the insurance company with a sub-standard group of lives.
> 
> There will also be an **[[Anti-selection]] risk**
> - When an applicant uses private knowledge of their own health to obtain a policy on terms that are financially favourable to them but disadvantages the insurer.

> [!trending-down] Investment risk
> Investment risk is a major factor for non-linked versions of [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/2.1 Whole life assurances|whole life insurance]].
> - The insurer faces uncertainty regarding the future rates of return that will be earned on premiums yet to be received. If the actual rate of return is lower than the rate assumed in the *pricing basis*, the insurer will make less profit than expected and if interest rates fall significantly, the accumulated proceeds may no longer be sufficient to meet the liabilities, potentially threatening the insurer's solvency.
> - For with-profits policies, this risk is somewhat lower because the insurer can reduce future bonuses if investment returns are poor, although guaranteed elements and P.R.E must still be met.
>

> [!receipt] Expense & inflation risk
> Whole life policies often have long durations (e.g. a policy written at age 30 may remain in force until age 90)
> - ***Inflation***: Fixed, level premiums have significant exposure to expense inflation
> - ***Administrative costs***: At very long durations, the actual cost of administering the policy may eventually exceed the premium collected (if premium cease at some age). 

> [!lapse] [[Withdrawal (Persistency) risk]]
> - ***Negative asset share***: Early withdrawals before initial expenses are recouped will lead the insurer to suffer a permanent loss.

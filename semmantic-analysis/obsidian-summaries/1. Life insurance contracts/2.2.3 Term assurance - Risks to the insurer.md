## Risks to the insurer

> [!skull] Mortality risk
> The main risk is that the number of deaths are higher than expected (or allowed for in the pricing basis).
> 
> - ***[[Anti-selection|Anti-selection risk]]***: in individual contracts, this is a big concern because policyholders can obtain large sum assured for relatively small premiums. The risk is that individuals who have private knowledge about their own poor health will be the most motivated to apply for cover.
> 
> - ***Random fluctuations***: Even if the underlying assumptions are correct, actual claims may exceed expectations due to unpredictable sample errors or sudden events like epidemics.
> - ***[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Selective withdrawal]]***: healthy lives are more likely to withdraw leaving the insurer with a sub-standard pool of lives who are more likely to claim.

> [!lapse] [[Withdrawal (Persistency) risk]]
> Specifically for **decreasing term assurance *with level premiums***, a policyholder may have a financial incentive to lapse an existing policy and take out a new one for the remaining term at a lower premium, which results in a loss for the original insurer.

> [!receipt] Expense risk
>
>- ***[[Marginal costs|Marginal costs]]***: there is a risk to the insurer that actual marginal costs for administering the contract will exceed those assumed. 
>- ***Fixed costs***: If sales volumes are lower than expected, fixed overheads must be spread over fewer policies, increasing the per-policy expense.
>- If [[Asset share]] is negative, the insurer suffers a loss on early withdrawal before initial expenses have been recouped.
>- ***Inflation***: Premiums are typically level and guaranteed, over longer years the insurer is exposed to expense inflation.

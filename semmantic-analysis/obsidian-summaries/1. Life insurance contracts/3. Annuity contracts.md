> [!note] Structural basis
> • **Conventional Without-Profits:** This is the oldest and most common form of annuity,. It provides **fully guaranteed benefits** in exchange for a single premium,. The insurer assumes the **longevity and investment risks** because the payment amounts are fixed in monetary terms from the outset,.
>
>• **Index-Linked:** The benefits of these contracts are linked to a specified **investment or economic index** (such as a retail price index) and move in line with that index's performance,. This structure is often used to **protect the policyholder's income against inflation**.
>
>• **With-Profits:** In this structure, the policyholder receives a **guaranteed amount plus a bonus** added by the insurer. The policyholder is entitled to a share of the **surplus earnings** of the with-profits fund. This basis sits between conventional and unit-linked contracts in terms of the risk carried by the insurer.
>
• **[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/4.1 Unit-linked contracts|Unit-linked]] (Unitised):** The benefits are **linked directly to the investment performance** of a specified fund. While the insurer may guarantee the payment of a specific number of units, the actual **monetary income is not guaranteed** because it fluctuates daily based on the unit price,. This structure passes the majority of the investment risk to the policyholder


> [!skull] Longevity risk
> - ***Mortality improvements***: The main risk is that annuitants live longer than the insurer allowed for in pricing, often due to *understating the rate of improvement* in life expectancy. 
> - ***Anti-selection***: When purchase is voluntary, those in good health or with a family history of longevity are most likely to buy an annuity (self-selection).

> [!trending-down] Investment risk
> - The insurer must invest the single premium to cover regular outgo over an **unknown timeframe**. 
> - This risk can be mitigated by matching liabilities with fixed-interest securities with redemption dates matching the amounts being paid each year.
> - In a perfectly matched strategy, assuming that annuitants die **exactly as expected**, the insurance company should not need to buy or sell any of its assets during the term of the contract because the cash needed is provided by the coupons and redemption amounts.
> - This removes **interest rate volatility risk** and yield uncertainty because the cashflows and yields are locked in at the time when the contract was priced. 
> - This scenario is impractical because:
> 	- *Timing*: The term of the annuity payments uncertain 
> 	- *Availability*: Assets with perfectly matching terms may not be available (reinvestment risk)
> 

> [!receipt] Expense risk
> All future expenses have to be met from the single premium.
> - **Expense reserve**: there is a risk that **future inflation** or **operational inefficiency** will make the initial reserve inadequate.
> - **Initial expenses** may be higher than allowed for in the pricing basis.

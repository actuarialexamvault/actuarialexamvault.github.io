
> [!check] Retirement income accumulation
> - According to [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/0. The personal financial lifecyle|the personal financial lifecycle]] saving for retirement becomes the biggest financial need for the individuals between the ages of 35 and 65. 

> [!check] Protection against longevity risk 
>-  Deferred annuities address the significant risk of **running out of money** if a policyholder becomes very old, this protects the individual's standard of living

> [!check] Flexibility in contributions 
>- A consumer may prefer the **flexibility of taking out a new single premium policy each year** based on their ability to pay. 
>- Unit-linked designs specifically address the need for flexibility by allowing policyholders to **miss premiums for ad hoc periods**.

> [!check] Retirement Transition and Debt Repayment 
>- At the "vesting date" (the end of the deferred period), these contracts address the need for liquidity by offering the option of a **lump sum in lieu of part or all of the pension**. 
>- This cash sum can be used to meet major financial obligations at the point of retirement, such as **paying off a house purchase loan**.

> [!check] Investment and Profit participation 
> Deferred annuities also serve as a **savings vehicle** where policyholders can choose the level of risk and reward they desire:
>- **With-Profits Basis:** Addresses the need for **smoothed investment returns** and a share in the insurer's profits through bonus additions during the deferment period.
>- **Unit-Linked Basis:** Meets the needs of consumers seeking **higher expected benefits** by taking on investment risk directly, often with a range of investment funds to choose from

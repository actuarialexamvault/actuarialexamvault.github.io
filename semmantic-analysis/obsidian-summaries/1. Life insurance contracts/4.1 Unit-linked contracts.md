#actuarial #f102 #life_insurance #definitions

> [!definition] Definition
> A **unit-linked contract** is a [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/1. Life insurance contracts|Life insurance]] policy where the benefits are **directly linked to the investment performance of a specified fund**. These contracts operate by paying policyholder premiums into **pooled investment funds** to purchase **"units,"** which represent a share of that fund.

>[!money-tree] Premiums:
>- Each premium under a unit-linked contract is paid into an investment fund and at that time is used to buy units at the offer price, which represent a share of that fund.
>- These units are added to the contract’s unit account. 

> [!receipt] Charges
>- The insurance company will deduct charges from the policyholders fund.
>- These may be deducted from the premiums before they are invested  (e.g. the allocation rate is less than 100%, bid-offer spread, fixed deduction from each premium).  
>- Charges may also be deducted form the unit funds. (e.g. fund management charge, regular fixed deduction, regular risk charges to cover risk benefits).
>- These charges will be met by the insurer cancelling units of an equal value to the charge levied.
>[!receipt] Initial expenses
>- Initial expenses can be recouped by having a very low, or zero allocation rate for a short period at the start of the contract
>- A reduced allocation rate (e.g. 93%) for a significant part of the policy term.
>- A higher regular fund charge:
>	- Through out the policy term or 
>	- Through special units (called capital units) at the start, these units would be subject to a higher fund charge than normal units.

> [!check] Benefits:
>- When an insured event happens the amount of the benefit is the then bid price value of all the units in the contract’s unit account.
>- This may be subject to a minimum guaranteed benefit, to provide a reasonable benefit at early durations when the fund value is relatively low. 
>- Where these cost more than the value of the unit fund at the claim date (as they usually would) then the insurer will meet the balance of cost.

> [!success] Maturity
>- The value of the policy at maturity is usually the bid value of units

> [!lapse] Surrender value
>- The [[Surrender Value]] will be equal to the bid-value of units minus a surrender penalty that may be deducted if the policyholder withdraws early.

>[!tip] Profit margin
>- The charges are used by the insurer to cover expenses and risk benefits, the insurer will aim for charges to exceed expenses and guaranteed benefits, to give profit to the insurer.

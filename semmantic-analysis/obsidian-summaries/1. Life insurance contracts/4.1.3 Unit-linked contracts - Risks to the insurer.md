The risks to an insurer are generally considered lower than those for non-linked contracts because most of the investment risk is passed to the policyholder. However several significant risks remain for the insurance company:

> [!receipt] Expense risk
>
>The risk is that **actual administration costs exceed the charges**. 
>- ***Mismatch of charges and costs***: The charges levied (expense, risk and management charges) may not match the actual expenses or the cost of guarantees (in terms of nature, timing and amount).
> 

> [!trending-down] Investment risk
>
>- Lower than expected returns lead to:
>	- lower than expected unit-linked charges resulting in non-recovery of expenses
>	- Higher than expected risk-charges, which further reduce the unit fund
>	- Lower than expected surrender/death/maturity benefits and disappointed policyholders (with possible reputational damage to the insurer)
>	- Poor unit-linked investment returns relative to competitors, resulting in selective withdrawals
>- If the insurer provides non-unit-linked guarantees (such as a minimum sum assured on death), and the unit fund falls below the guaranteed level, the insurer must meet the shortfall

> [!skull] Mortality risk
> - ***Minimum guaranteed death benefits***: the insuer is at risk if the mortality risk charges collected are insufficient to cover these claims.

> [!lapse] [[Withdrawal (Persistency) risk]]
> - ***Negative asset share***: There is a financial risk if a policyholder surrenders early, when the [[Asset share]] is negative or lower than the surrender value, and the unit fund is too small for a surrender penalty to fully offset the loss. 
>- ***[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Selective withdrawal]]***: is higher for unit-linked contracts than [[Why selective withdrawal risk is higher for unit-linked contracts|comparable non-linked versions]].

> [!note] [[External risks]]
>- ***Marketing and reputational risk***: Because these contracts have lower guarantees, they produce greater uncertainty of return for the policyholder. If investment performance is poor, it can lead to **policyholder dissatisfaction, bad publicity, and reduced future sales**, especially if the risks were not clearly understood at the time of purchase.
>- **Marketing pressures, competition, or legislative restrictions** may prevent an insurer from increasing reviewable charges enough to cover deteriorating expense performance
>- ***Changes in Legislation***: There is a risk that regulatory changes could restrict an insurer's ability to hold negative non-unit reserves or use actuarial funding to reduce capital strain (taking advance credit for future contributions toward initial expenses that were already incurred)

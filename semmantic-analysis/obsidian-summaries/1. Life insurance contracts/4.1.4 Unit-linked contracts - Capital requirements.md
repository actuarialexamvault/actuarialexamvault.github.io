
> [!solvency] Capital requirements
> |**Driver**|**Comment on Impact**|
|---|---|
|**Contract Design**|**Critical**. Design determines how quickly capital is recouped; for instance, allocating a very low or zero percentage of early premiums to units reduces unit-related liabilities and supervisory reserves, making the contract very **capital efficient**.|
|**Premium Payment Frequency**|**High**. **Single premium contracts** require the least capital because all funds are received immediately; **regular monthly premiums** require the most capital because the initial expenses are high relative to the first small premium received.|
|**Pricing vs. Supervisory Reserving Bases**|**High**. The greater the gap between these two bases, the higher the resulting **initial capital strain**.|
|**Additional Solvency Capital Requirements**|**Low**. Unit-linked business typically has **lower solvency margin requirements** than traditional business because it provides fewer investment guarantees..|
|**Level of Initial Expenses**|**High**. Higher initial costs for marketing, commission, and underwriting increase the capital requirement.|
|**Non-Unit-Linked Guarantees**|**High. Offering** guarantees not linked to unit value (e.g., a **minimum maturity value** or guaranteed annuity rate) significantly increases the insurer's direct investment risk and the resulting capital needed to back those promises,.|
>|**Valuation Standards and Regulation**|**Moderate**. Regulatory changes may restrict an insurer's ability to hold **negative non-unit reserves** or use actuarial funding to reduce capital strain. For example, some jurisdictions specify that the total sum of all non-unit reserves **must not be negative**.|

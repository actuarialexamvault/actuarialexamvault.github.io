
> [!danger] Risk to the policyholder
> |**Product Basis**|**Investment / Market Risk**|**Inflation Risk**|**Counterparty (Insolvency) Risk**|**Flexibility & Need Risk**|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**[[Conventional]] [[Without-Profits (Non-Participating)]]**|**Low**. Benefits are fixed and guaranteed at the outset.|**High**. Fixed monetary benefits are likely to be eroded by inflation over the long term, becoming "insufficient".|**High**. The insured is entirely dependent on the insurer’s ability to meet the full fixed guarantee.|**High**. These products are typically inflexible to changing disposable income or benefit needs.|
|**[[With-Profits (Participating)]]**|**Moderate**. Policyholders bear some risk through potential reductions in non-guaranteed bonuses.|**Moderate**. Bonus additions provide some protection against inflation, though it is not guaranteed.|**Low**. Future surpluses can be used to maintain company solvency before being distributed to policyholders.|**Moderate**. Risk of "disappointment" if final payouts do not fulfil expectations or projected needs.|
|**Unit-Linked**|**High**. The insured bears the primary investment risk; poor fund performance directly reduces the benefit.|**Moderate**. Investing in real assets (equities/property) can hedge inflation, but monetary guarantees remain exposed.|**Moderate**. The policyholder carries the risk that the insurer may be unable to meet its obligations.|**Low**. High flexibility to vary premiums and benefits as needs change over time.|
|**Index-Linked**|**High**. Depends on the performance of the specified index.|**Low**. Specifically designed to protect the real value of benefits if linked to a price index. However, does not match rate of individual spending inflation|**High**. The insurer bears more investment risk than in unit-linked, increasing insolvency risk.|**High**. These products generally offer much less flexibility than unit-linked designs.|

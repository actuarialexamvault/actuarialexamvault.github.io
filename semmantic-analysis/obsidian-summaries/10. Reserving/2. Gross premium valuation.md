
> [!definition] GPV Definition
> 
> This is a method for placing a value on a life insurance company’s liabilities that **explicitly** values of **expenses** and **claims** including future discretionary benefits less **future office premiums**.

^ed67b4

> [!special] What are the features of the gross premium method?
> - **Liabilities**: All liabilities to policyholders arising from the contract should be allowed for:
> 	- Future expenses
> 	- Future benefits and expected future bonuses
> 	- Guarantees and options 
> 	- Guaranteed surrender values (the reserve > surrender value)
> 	- Future office premiums
> - **Term**: Projection to end of policy duration
> - **Assumptions**: 
> 	- Mortality rates and lapse rates, based on the policyholder profile
> 	- Discount rate based on the yields of assets matching the liabilities or the risk-free rate (market-consistent valuation)
> - **Negative reserves**: realistic basis could produce **negative reserves**, however negative reserves are zeroised for solvency purposes 
> - **Sensitivity**: Sensitive to changes in the valuation basis, because the office premium used is based on initial pricing basis
> - **Surplus emergence**: Profit emerges gradually over the duration, the valuation discount rate must be consistent with future bonus rates
> 

>[!info] How are reserves calculated under the **GPV** method?
>Reserves for any policy are calculated as:
>$$\text{EPV(Benefits)} + \text{EPV(Expenses)} - \text{EPV(Premiums)}$$

> [!math] What is the GPV formula method?
> For a standard regular premium without-profits endowment assurance, the reserve at duration _t_ is expressed as:
>
> $$V_t = SA \cdot A_{x+t:\overline{n-t|}} + RE \cdot \ddot{a}_{x+t:\overline{n-t|}} - P' \cdot \ddot{a}_{x+t:\overline{n-t|}}$$
>
>- **SA (Sum Assured):** The contractual benefit to be paid.
>- **RE (Renewal Expenses):** The expected future costs to maintain the policy.
>- **P' (Office Premium):** The actual gross premium the policyholder is expected to pay
>
>An alternative to the formula approach is to use a cashflow approach where the net cashflows are calculated for each future period and then discounted back.

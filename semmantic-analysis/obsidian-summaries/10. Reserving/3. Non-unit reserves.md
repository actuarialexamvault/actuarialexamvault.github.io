
> [!summary] What is a non-unit reserve?
> Due to the complexity of unit linked-contracts a cashflow approach is necessary. We refer to the gross premium cashflow reserve of a unit-linked contract as the non-unit reserve.
> $$\text{Non-unit reserve} = \text{EPV[Non-unit Outgo]} - \text{EPV[Non-unit Income]}$$
> Where:
>- ***Non-unit outgo***: includes future costs, such as administration expenses, benefit outgo in excess of the unit fund.
>- ***Non-unit income***: includes unallocated premiums, fund management charges, risk charges and bid-offer spreads

> [!solvency] How is the non-unit reserve calculated in a Prudential valuation?
> The non-unit reserve is defined as the amount required to ensure that the company is able to pay claims and expenses **without needing further financing**. 
> 
> The company projects forwards its year-by-year non-unit cashflows on the reserving basis and works backwards from the end of the policy term.
> 1. Project cashflows
> 2. Identify the last negative cashflow
> 3. Zeroise the shortfall by holding the required money at the start of that period and allowing for investment returns during the year.
> 4. Deduct from the cashflow at end of the previous period.
> 5. Repeat backward.
>
>At the end of the **zeroization** process (valuation date), if the discounted adjusted cashflow is negative then a non-unit reserve is set up equal to the absolute value of that negative amount. In a prudential valuation if the discounted adjusted cashflow is non-negative the non-unit reserve is typically set to zero.

> [!best-estimate] How is the non-unit reserve calculated in a Best estimate valuation?
>- ***Recognises net inflows***: A best estimate valuation would not disregard future non-unit cashflows occurring after the last projection period in which there is a negative cashflow (net outflow). 
>- ***Negative non-unit reserves***: under a best estimate valuation negative non-unit reserves can be held.


> [!definition] What is the Net premium valuation method?
> Under the net premium valuation method, policy reserves are calculated as:
> $$Reserve = \text{EPV( Future benefit outgo)} - \text{EPV(Future Net premiums)}$$ 
>
>Properties
>- **Assumptions**: The present values are calculated using ***only interest and mortality assumptions***
>- ***Net premium***: is a theoretical premium calculated at policy inception to cover only the guaranteed benefits (ignoring expenses and future bonuses), calculated using the  **current interest and mortality basis as the valuation**
>- ***No explicit allowance for future bonuses***
>- ***No explicit allowance for expenses.***

> [!in-flow] What are the benefits of using the net premium method?
> - ***Simplicity***: it is straight forward and has minimum data requirements
> - ***Implicit margin***: for regular premium business it relies on implicit allowances. Expenses and future bonuses are intended to be covered by the margin between the actual office premium and the theoretical net premium.
> - ***Low sensitivity to basis changes*** because the net premium is recalculated on the valuation basis whenever the assumptions change, which partially offsets the impact on the reserve.
> - ***Regional usage***: it is the prescribed valuation method in certain African countries.

> [!out-flow] What are the limitations of using the net premium method?
> - Not appropriate for **single premium business** because there are no future premiums to provide implicit margins for expenses or bonuses
> - **Lack of bonus support**: it often requires very low valuation rate of interest to ensure a suitable implicit allowance for future bonuses.
> - ***No capitalisation of future profits***
> 

> [!math] What is the net premium formula?
Under the **net premium valuation method**, the reserve for a **regular premium** **with-profits endowment assurance** with constant premiums is calculated using the following formula:
>
>$$\text{Reserve} = (S + B) A_{x+t:\overline{n-t|}} - P \ddot{a}_{x+t:\overline{n-t|}}$$
>**Components:**
>
>- **$S$**: The initial **sum assured**.
>- **$B$**: The amount of **bonus declared to date** (this excludes explicit allowance for future bonuses).
>- **$P$**: The **net premium**, which is the theoretical premium calculated at policy inception to cover only the initial guaranteed benefits, ignoring expenses. $$P = \frac{S \cdot A_{x:\overline{n|}}}{\ddot{a}_{x:\overline{n|}}}$$
>- **$A_{x+t:\overline{n-t|}}$**: The expected present value of a benefit of 1 payable at the end of the year of death or at maturity.
>- **$\ddot{a}_{x+t:\overline{n-t|}}$**: The expected present value of an annuity of 1 per year payable in advance for the remainder of the term or until earlier death.

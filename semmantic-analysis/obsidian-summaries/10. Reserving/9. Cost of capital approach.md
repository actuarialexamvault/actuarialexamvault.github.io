
>[!definition] Cost of capital approach
The **cost of capital approach** is a methodology used primarily to determine a **risk margin** within a market-consistent valuation of insurance liabilities. It represents the compensation a market participant would require for the cost of holding capital against [[../../Definitions/Non-hedgeable risks]].

> [!cycle] **The Three-Step Calculation Process**
>1. **Project Required Capital:** The insurer projects the future capital required to be held $(C_t​)$ for each period during the "run-off" of the business. (this is the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/11. Capital requirements/1. SCR|SCR]])
>2. **Apply Cost of Capital Rate:** These projected capital amounts are multiplied by a **cost of capital rate** (e.g., 6% per annum).
>3. **Discount to Present Value:** The resulting annual costs are discounted back to the valuation date using **risk-free (market-consistent) interest rates** to produce the total risk margin. $$\text{Risk margin} = \displaystyle{\sum_t \dfrac{k_tC_t}{(1 + r_t)^t}}$$

>[!percent] **Cost of Capital Rate**
>The rate (often specified by regulators) represents two main concepts:
>
>- **Incremental Cost:** The cost of raising additional capital in excess of the risk-free rate.
>
>- **Frictional/Opportunity Cost:** The cost to the company of "locking in" capital to earn a low risk-free rate instead of investing it freely for higher rewards (such as in equities) or paying it out to shareholders.

>[!steps] **Applications in Actuarial Work**
>
>• **Market-Consistent Valuation:** It values the uncertain aspects of liability cashflows (like mortality or expenses) for which there is no active trading market.
>
>• **Embedded Value (EV):** In market-consistent frameworks, this approach is used to deduct a risk margin from the EV to reflect the cost of capital tied up in supervisory reserves.
>
>• **Asset Shares:** A related concept is used where deductions are made from a policy's asset share to compensate the company (or other policyholders) for the "loan" provided to fund its initial **new business strain**.

> [!trajectories] **Simplified Approaches**
>
>Because projecting precise capital requirements for every future year is complex, insurers may use **drivers** (like projected reserves or sum at risk) that have a linear relationship with capital to approximate future requirements

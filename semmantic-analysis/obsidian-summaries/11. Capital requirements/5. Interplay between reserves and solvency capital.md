
> [!solvency] Discuss the interplay between supervisory reserves and solvency capital
> The relationship between supervisory reserves and solvency capital is **interdependent**, the adequacy of one cannot be assessed in isolation from the other. These two components are balanced in one of two ways:
> - ***Prudent reserving***: reserves are calculated with large margins for error, and paired with relatively small, fixed solvency capital requirements (e.g. in a [[../10. Reserving/5. Net premium valuation|Net premium valuation]])
> - ***Realistic reserving***: Reserves are set to **best estimate** values with small margins but the insurer must hold a substantial risk-based solvency capital.
>
>While the labels differ, the total assets required (reserves plus solvency capital) are often similar across jurisdictions to ensure a high degree of policyholder security.
>
>A higher total requirement, whether through stronger reserves or higher capital margins will always increase the [[../../Definitions/New Business Strain|initial capital strain]], for new policies. This reduces a company's [[../../Definitions/Free Assets|free assets]] which are the funds left over to finance new business or support a riskier investment strategy.
>


> [!sum] What are the methods of calculating surrender values?
> 1. Retrospective method
> 2. Prospective method
> 3. Blended method
> 4. Unit-linked (Bid value less penalty) method

> [!max] Explain the retrospective method
> An assessment of the **[[../../Definitions/Asset share|asset share]]** or the **accumulated net cashflow** associated with a policy to date.
> - identifies the **maximum value** a company can pay **without incurring a financial loss** 
> - ensures values do not appear unreasonably low relative to premiums at early durations
> - used at early durations where initial expenses dominate the asset share
> 
> Example: $$SV = \frac{D_x}{D_{x+t}} \left[ G \cdot \ddot{a}^{(m)}_{x:t} - SA \cdot A^1_{x:t} - I - e \cdot \ddot{a}_{x:t} - f \cdot A^1_{x:t} \right] - C$$
> 

> [!floor] Explain the prospective method
> The discounted value of future benefits and expenses, net of future premiums using best estimate assumptions.
> - ensures the surrender value progresses smoothly into the maturity value
> - allows the company to  capitalize future profits
> - used primarily after the initial early stage of the policy.
> - It is very sensitive to future assumptions, particularly the interest rate assumption
>
>Example: $$SV = SA \cdot A_{x+t} + e \cdot \ddot{a}_{x+t} + f \cdot A_{x+t} - G \cdot \ddot{a}^{(m)}_{x+t} - C$$

> [!exponential] Explain the blended method
> It is common to use retrospective values at early durations and transition to prospective values at later durations.
> - used to balance the need for expense recovery early on with the need for consistency with maturity values later
> - merges a retrospective calculation into a prospective one

> [!trending-up] Explain the unit-linked method
> For unit-linked business, the calculation is simpler: $$SV = \text{Bid value of allocated units} - \text{Surrender penalty}$$
> - Used to recoup initial expenses
> - Ensures the company makes the intended profit required by shareholders
> - Surrender values tend to be large at early durations and decrease over time

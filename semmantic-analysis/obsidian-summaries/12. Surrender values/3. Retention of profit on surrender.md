
> [!question] Why do companies retain profit on surrenders?
> To ensure the company achieves its original profit requirements and return on capital
> To prevent "lapse and re-entry" scenarios
> To maintain equity between policyholders who leave and those who continue

> [!trajectories] What is a suitable path for a surrender value:
>-  **Initially**: No surrender value is granted.
>- **Early stage**: The value is often subject to a minimum of the premiums paid.
>- **Intermediate stage**: The value increases in line with the **earned asset share** until the company determines it is reasonable to begin retaining future profit.
>- **Final stage**: The amount of profit retained increases so that, as maturity approaches, the surrendering contract becomes as profitable for the insurer **as if it had remained in force** until the end.

> [!receipt] How does a company determine the profit retained if a policy surrenders?
> It would take the difference between the **[[../../Definitions/Asset share|asset share]]** ($AS$) and the **surrender value** ($SV$) paid to the policyholder: $$Profit = AS - SV$$
> If it adopts a [[2. Methods of calculation#^474da8|blended approach]] the profit retained can be defined as: $$Profit = (AS - SV') + (SV' - SV'')$$
> where: 
> - $SV'$ - prospective surrender value using same assumptions as used to calculate the office premium
> - $SV''$ - prospective surrender value using **surrender value basis** assumptions
> 
>Here the profit consists of two distinct financial components:
>- ***Accrued profit***: The profit that has been made to date
>- ***Capitalized future profit***: The present value of future profits that will no longer be received because the policy terminated early.

> [!question] How does the choice of surrender basis influence how much future profit is extracted/capitalized?
>  The profit on surrender is equal to the [[../../Definitions/Asset share|Asset share]] per policy less the surrender benefit paid.
> The profit on surrender is zero when the ***surrender benefit is equal to the earned asset share***.
> 
>The capitalized value of future profit arises from the differences between the premium rate assumptions and the surrender values assumptions.
>- ***Best estimate basis***: If the insurer uses realistic, best estimate assumptions for calculating the surrender value, it effectively retains the same total profit it would have made if the contract stayed in force until the end
>- ***Premium basis***: If the insurer uses the original premium basis (with margins), it only retains the profit made up to date.
>- ***Blended basis***: Capitalising future profits may be considered unfair to surrendering policyholders so many companies use a "blended" approach, which is a basis between the realistic basis and the premium basis

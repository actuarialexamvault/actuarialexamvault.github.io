
> [!max] How to determine the basis for a retrospective surrender value?
> To determine the basis for a retrospective surrender value, and insurer examines its **actual past experience** for the specific policy or group of policies.
> - ***Investment earnings***: The actual rates of return earned on the backing assets, which may be smoothed over time for regular premium contracts.
> - ***Expenses***: The actual marginal acquisition and administration costs incurred to date
> - ***Mortality/morbidity***: The actual claims experience
> - ***Taxation***: any tax paid on investment income or capital gains attributable to the policy
>
>Insurers may deviate from "true" experience for specific reasons, such as:
>- **Profit retention:** They might an investment return lower than actual to ensure the company retains a profit margin
>- **Marketability**: They might deduct lower than actual expenses if the true asset share would results in an embarrassingly low value for the policyholder

> [!floor] How to determine the basis for a prospective surrender value?
>Determining a prospective surrender basis involves selecting assumptions for **future experience** that balance profit retention with policyholder equity. 
>- Interest rate: The most critical parameter. It is typically set based on the current weighted average **[[../../Definitions/Redemption yield|redemption yield]]** of matching fixed-interest securities
>- Expenses: Derived from the company's latest expense investigation. 
>	- Includes the costs of processing the surrender
>	- renewal expenses and commissions 
>	- **excludes prudential margins**.
>- Mortality: reflects the expected lighter mortality of those who choose to leave.
>	- select mortality could be used (although the selective process is not the same as that of the standard table used)
>	- use unadjusted mortality and rely on other margins in the basis for diferrences in the mortality of leavers.
>- Inflation: set consistently with investment returns i.e. [[../../Explainers/The link between inflation and investment returns|The link between inflation and investment returns]]
>	- price-inflation and earnings inflation

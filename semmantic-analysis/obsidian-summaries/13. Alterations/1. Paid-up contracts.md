
> [!definition] What is a paid-up contract?
> A **paid-up** contract is a specific type of alteration where the policyholder stops paying premiums, but instead of taking a cash surrender value, the policy continues with a reduced "paid-up" sum assured.

> [!info] What are the key principles when determining the paid-up sum assured?
> ***Ongoing Costs***: The costs of making a policy paid-up for the insurer is different from those of paying a surrender value because of ongoing administration expenses. 
> ***Affordability***: The new benefit must be supported by the policy's earned asset share to ensure the insurer doesn't suffer a loss.
> ***Consistency with surrender values***: The paid-up value should be consistent with the surrender value. This prevents policyholders from "gaming" the system by converting to paid-up status and then immediately surrendering for a higher amount.
> **Maturity value**: The paid-up sum assured should progress smoothly towards the final maturity value as the policy nears its end.

> [!exponential] What are the key assumption changes when a policy becomes paid-up?
> - ***Removal of renewal commission***: Since no further premiums are being collected, which increases the benefit to the policyholder
> - ***Higher Mortality***: Insurers may assume slightly higher mortality for paid-up lives than those who surrender, as people in poor health are less likely to give up their cover entirely.

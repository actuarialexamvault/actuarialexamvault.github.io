
> [!info] What are are the key principles for setting terms for general alterations?
>- **Affordability**: The new terms must be backed by the [[asset share]] of the contract
>- **Boundary conditions**: consistency with surrender, paid-up or new policy actions
>- **Stability** in benefits and premiums 
>- **Simplicity** Ease of calculation and of explanation to the policyholder.
>- **Incentives**: Avoidance of lapse and re-entry
>- **Profitability**: the insurer shouldn't make a financial loss on the alteration.
>- **Expenses**: The new policy value should deduct the expenses of altrenation
>- **F**requency of Alterations
>
>**A.B.S.S.I.P.E.F**

> [!max] How are boundary conditions used to assess proposals for alterations?
> [[../../Definitions/Boundary conditions|Boundary conditions]] act as limits or tests to verify that proposed changes are fair and don't allow policyholders to exploit inconsistencies in the company's pricing and terms.
> - ***Surrender***: A reduction in a policy's term to zero should produce a value consistent with the cash surrender value
> - ***Paid-up status***: A reduction in sum assured should be consistent with a paid-up calculation
> - ***New policy***: For an increase in benefits, the additional premium should be consistent with what the company would charge for taking out a new policy of that size.
> - **Alteration to itself**: reflecting a minor change

> [!example] How does a company ensure prevent financial loss on an alteration?
> The new terms must be supported by the **[[../../Definitions/Asset share|asset share]]** of the policy before alteration to insurer that the insurer doesn't suffer a financial loss.

> [!receipt] How does a company ensure fairness in profitability after an alteration?
> The expected profit after an alteration should be consistent with the expected profit before the change or with the amount if the policy were originally written on the new terms.

>[!Example] State the most relevant boundary condition in each of the following cases.
>|Case|Relevant Boundary Condition|Reason / Context|
|---|---|---|
|**Term assurance altered to endowment assurance**|New policy|The existing policy value is likely very small.|
|**Endowment assurance premium reduced to a quarter level**|Paid-up policy|The reduction in premium is significant, approaching a premium-free state.|
|**Endowment assurance sum assured quadrupled**|New policy|Large increases in cover are analogous to purchasing an additional increment policy.|
|**Endowment assurance term reduced to 2 years** (15 years in out of 35)|Surrender|The remaining term effectively becomes zero, which is the limiting case of a term reduction.|
|**Endowment assurance term reduced by 2 years** (15 years in out of 35)|Original policy itself|This is a minor change; the method should remain stable and reproduce existing terms for small adjustments .|


> [!cycle] What methods are available for valuing an investment guarantee?
> - Option-pricing techniques
> - Stochastic simulation of investment performance

> [!exponential] How are option pricing techniques used to value guarantees?
> This approach treats insurance guarantees as analogous to market-traded derivatives. In these scenarios the insurer is seen as a seller of the option to the policyholder (in exchange for a premium), who can then exercise it to limit their downside own risk. 
> - ***Maturity guarantees***: valued as [[../../../../UNISA/DSC4825 - Mathematical finance 1/Summaries/1.4 Options#^c81634|European-style]] [[../../../../UNISA/DSC4825 - Mathematical finance 1/Summaries/1.4 Options#^f30c2d|put options]].
> - ***Surrender guarantees***: valued as [[../../../../UNISA/DSC4825 - Mathematical finance 1/Summaries/1.4 Options#^4ac09a|American-style]] [[../../../../UNISA/DSC4825 - Mathematical finance 1/Summaries/1.4 Options#^f30c2d|put options]].
> - ***Guaranteed annuity rates***: valued as [[../../../../UNISA/DSC4825 - Mathematical finance 1/Summaries/1.4 Options#^334e32|call options]] on bonds or [[../../../../UNISA/DSC4825 - Mathematical finance 1/Summaries/7. Swaps#^9158bb|swaps]] option (to swap floating rate returns for fixed rate returns)
>
>Under market-consistent valuations, if suitable options exists in the market that replicates the cashflows from the guarantee, the market prices of those are taken as the value of the option (Market-to-market)
>
>Formulas for option values like **Black-Scholes** are also used to calculate the values of the options efficiently. They are helpful for avoiding [[../../Definitions/Nested simulations|nested simulations]]. 

>[!distribution] How are investment guarantees valued using stochastic simulation?
>- Identify the underlying assets
>- ***Project asset values***: use a stochastic model to simulate future asset prices or returns based on a chosen probability distribution (e.g. mean and variance) for the underlying assets
>- ***Model Policyholder behaviour:*** Incorporate assumptions about when policyholders are likely to exercise the option (when it is in the money).
>- ***Identify shortfalls:*** In each simulation, compare the projected fund value against the guaranteed amount. If the fund is lower, the difference is the cost of the guarantee for that specific scenario. For paths where the fund value is above the guarantee, the cost is recorded as zero.
>- **Discounting**: Discount the cost of each individual path back to the valuation date e.g. using the risk-free rate under market consistent frameworks
>- ***Expected cost***: Take the average of the simulated costs across all runs, which provides the expected present value for the guarantee,
>

> [!info] How would we modify the stochastic method for different situations?
> - Guaranteed surrender values at the end of the year:
> 	- Identify the first time in each simulation the guarantee is in the money
> - Conventional without profit: 
> 	- Project the asset share for a given premium and compare against the guarantee (basic sum assured) 
> - Guaranteed annuity rate:
> 	- Project future bond yields and calculate the actual cost of providing the annuity in each simulation vs the guaranteed annuity amount.

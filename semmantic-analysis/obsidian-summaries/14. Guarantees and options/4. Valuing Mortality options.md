
> [!exchange] How are mortality options valued?
> Mortality options can be valued using cashflow projections. 
> 
> The insurer models the:
> - Probability the person **survives** to the option date
> - Probability they **choose to exercise** the option
> - **Expected future mortality** of people who exercise
>- The cost of the option is the **extra cover at standard rates**.
>
>The extra premium to charge for the option must cover:
>	- The expected cost of the option above
>	- Expenses
>	- Profit margin

> [!question] What are the extra assumptions the insurer would need to make?
> - ***Option take-up rates***: the probability that the policyholder will exercise the option at each available date
> 	- Conventional approach: assumes 100% of eligible policyholder will take the maximum benefit
> 	- Realistic approach: uses percentages based on past experience
> - ***Mortality of exercisers***: Due to anti-selection, those who exercise the option are assumed to have heavier mortality than standard lives. (e.g. 150% of the base table)
> - ***Mortality of non-exercisers***: assume lower than standard mortality so that the weighted average amounts to the original best estimate level. 
> - ***Benefit choice***: if the policyholder has a choice of benefit levels, an assumption is made about which level will be chosen (often assume the most expensive option for the insurer)
> - ***Additional expenses***: Allow for the costs of notifying policyholders of their option and the administrative work to process the change.

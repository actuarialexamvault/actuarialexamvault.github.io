
> [!math] How to derive risk premium rates for critical illness contracts?
> The overall incidence rate ($i_x$) is the **sum of incidence rates** for each defined condition (e.g., heart attack, stroke, cancer, other) minus any interactions between causes.$$i_x = i_x^{hd} + i_x^s + i_x^c + i_x^o - k_x^{hd}i_x^s$$
> The final risk premium depends on the policy structure:
> - ***Standalone CI:*** The cost is the incidence rate multiplied by the probability of surviving the [[../../Definitions/Survival period|survival period]]. $$i_x \times \text{Probability of surviving survival period}$$
> - ***Accelerated CI***: This pays on the earlier of death or diagnosis. The rate combines the CI incidence rate with the mortality rate ($qx$), adjusted for deaths already counted as CI events ($k_xq_x$).$$i_x + (1 - k_x)q_x$$
> 
> The final risk premium is found by multiplying $i_x$ by the sum assured $S$.

> [!info] Discuss the sources of data to derive critical illness rates?
>Data sources
>- ***Population data:*** general population medical records can be adjusted for a select insured population with higher income and education levels
>- ***Internal data***: is often insufficient for newer or less common conditions.
>- ***Industry and reinsurer data***: Pooled data from multiple insurers is often inconsistent because different companies cover different illnesses and use varying claim definitions
>
>Other general data issues
>- ***Data Scarcity***: because CI is a relatively new product in many markets historical experience is often unreliable or non-existent.
>- ***Credibility***: sufficient data only exists for major conditions while other conditions lack the volume required to make reliable assumptions.
>- **Rapid obsolescence**: Medical advances in screening and treatment quickly change incidence rates, making past data less relevant for future projections.


> [!cycle] How to derive risk premium rates for LTCI using a multi-state model?
> $$EPV(Benefits) =  \sum_{j=1}^n S_j \cdot \left(\int_0^{\infty} v^t \cdot {}_tp_x^{0j} \right) dt$$
> This methods tracks policyholders through several distinct states $(0,1,2,3,..)$, such as ***0:healthy, 1:needing home care, 2:nursing home care and 3:dead***.
> 
>- Instead of one benefit $S$, we use $S_j$, the specific benefit amount for care state $j$
> 
>- The probability ${}_tp_x^{0j}$ represents the likelihood of being in care state $j$ at time $t$. $${}_tp_x^{0j} = \int_0^t {}_r p_x^{00} \cdot \mu_{x+r}^{0j} \cdot {}_{t-r}p_{x+r}^{jj} dr$$
>- In LTCI models, the transition intensity to recovery is typically assumed to be zero, meaning the term ${}_{t-r}p_{x+r}^{jj}$ accounts only for mortality while in that specific care state 

> [!math] How is the inception / disability annuity model used in LTCI?
> The adjusted integral is: $$EPV(Benefit) = \sum_{j=1}^n \int_0^\infty v^t \cdot {}_tp_x^{00} \cdot \mu_{x+t}^{0j} \cdot (\Delta S_{j} \cdot \overline{a}_{x+t}^{j}) dt$$
>
>- **Summation over States ($\sum_{j=1}^n$):** Because LTCI involves multiple care levels, you must sum the expected costs across all $n$ defined care states.
>- **Incremental Benefits ($\Delta S_j$):** In LTCI, move to a higher tier (e.g., from Home Care to Nursing Home) triggers only the **additional increment** in benefit rather than a full new payment 
>- **State-Specific Inception ($\mu_{x+t}^{0j}$):** This represents the force of transition from the "Healthy" state to the specific care level $j$.
>- **Impaired Life Annuity ($\overline{a}_{x+t}^{j}$):** This is a whole-life impaired annuity. Unlike Income Protection, LTCI models typically assume **recovery is impossible**, meaning the annuity only terminates upon death.


> [!definition] 
> Original terms reinsurance (also known as coinsurance) involves a **proportional sharing** of all aspects of an insurance contract between the insurer (cedant) and the reinsurer. The insurance company sets the premium, and the reinsurance premium is in direct proportion to this. 
> 
> The amount of commission agreed with the reinsurer sets the net cost of the reinsurance arrangement, and is usually very significant.

> [!trajectories] What are the different allocation methods for the reinsured amount?
>- **Quota share**: a fixed percentage per policy
>- **Individual surplus**: the excess of the policy benefit over the insurer's retention limit

> [!info] What are the key features of OTR?
>Pros
>- Proportional sharing of premiums and claims, therefore all risks are shared
>- Reinsurance commission (Profit sharing) arrangements
>- Reduces new business strain through initial commission paid by the reinsurer
>
>Limitations
>- Difficult to obtain for **with-profits** business because the reinsurer must follow the insurer's discretionary bonus rates
>- Difficult to arrange for **unit-linked** business because reinsurer cannot easily match unit liabilities tied to an insurer's internal investment funds.

> [!money-tree] In what ways might a reinsurer be more attractive to the insurer?
>- Offer **higher commissions**
>- **Great financial strength** to reduce the insurer's credit risk of the insurer defaulting
>- **Broader coverage**, willingness to accept higher benefit limits or automatic acceptance limits
>- **Technical assistance** offering expertise in underwriting, product design and systems
>- **Profit-sharing**: experience-refund arrangements where the insurer receives a share of any profits the reinsurer makes from the ceded portfolio
>- **Financial reinsurance** support (e.g. nil premium payment)
>- Offer **lower risk premium rates** by leveraging **tax advantages or lower capital** requirements

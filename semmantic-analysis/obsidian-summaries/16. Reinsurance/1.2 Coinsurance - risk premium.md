
> [!definition] 
>Risk premium reinsurance (also known as the "recurring single premium" approach) involves the reinsurer setting its **own premium rates** to cover a specific portion of the risk

> [!question] What are the key characteristics of RPR?
>- The reinsurer sets the reinsurance premium rate
>- The risk premium operates as **age-rated recurring single premiums** that change over time as the policyholder ages and the amount of risk fluctuates.
>- **Reinsured amount** can be the full sum assured (term) or the sum at risk (for whole life or endowment)
>- Premium rates may be annually **reviewable or guaranteed** for a given term.
>- The risk premiums will vary from period to period due to changes in the sum at risk, age of policyholder, or as a result of rate reviews.
>- The reinsurance commission is usually **not significant**.
>- **Unit-linked application** because it aligns with the mortality charges deducted from unit funds
>- **Reserves** used to calculate the sum at risk will be specified in the treaty 

> [!trajectories] What are the different allocation methods for the reinsured amount?
>- **Quota share**: a fixed percentage per policy, e.g. 60% of sum at risk


> [!definition]
> Excess of loss is a non-proportional form of reinsurance where the reinsurer pays for losses that exceed a predetermined retention limit.

> [!question] What are the main types of XL Re?
> - Catastrophe: Covers non-independent risk, such as an industrial accident affecting multiple members of a group life scheme
> - Stop loss: Caps the total aggregate loss on an entire portfolio over a specific period, usually one year.

> [!steps] What are the key characteristics of XL Re?
>- Non-proportional, the reinsurance **premiums paid are not proportional** to the amount of claims payable under the treaty.
>- The sum reinsured or potential claim amount is **not known** in advance.
>- There is typically an **upper limit** on the ultimate cover provided.
>- Treaties are often organised into **lines or layers**, where the insurer might retain the first layer of losses (below the retention limit), the second layer will have one price, the next layer over the second will have a different price and so on. 
>- **Different reinsurers** may then take different proportions of each line.
>- Within lines, the reinsurer may also pay only a **percentage of the loss** actually incurred e.g. 90%
>- **Renewability** of cover is subject to renegotiation usually on an annual basis.

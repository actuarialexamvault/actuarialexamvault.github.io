
> [!definition]
> Fin Re are contracts that are primarily used as a tool for capital management and improving an insurer's solvency position rather than for transferring actual insurance risk.

> [!info] What are the main types of Fin Re?
>- **Financing arrangements**: The reinsurer provides a substantial initial commission to finance [[../../Definitions/New Business Strain|new business strain]] these loans are repaid through higher future reinsurance premiums.
>- **Contingent loans**: the reinsurer provides a loan where repayments are contingent on future profits (VIF) being generated by a specific block of business. Because repayments are not guaranteed the insurer may not have to show the loan as a liability, immediately increasing its supervisory solvency position.
>  

> [!question] What are the key considerations for Fin Re?
>- **Capitalization of future profit**: it reduces the risk that future profits won't materialize by converting them into immediate cash.
>- **Regulation:** its effectiveness is decreasing in jurisdictions moving toward realistic or market consistent reporting, where credit for future profits is already recognized or where loan repayments must be reserved as liabilities.
>- **No impact on value**: while it improves the balance sheet, it generally has little effect on the insurers realistic embedded value, as the immediate cash injection is offset by the expected cost of future repayments.
>

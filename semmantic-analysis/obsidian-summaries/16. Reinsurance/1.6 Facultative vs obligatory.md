
> [!definition]
> These terms describe the level of freedom or obligation each party has in a reinsurance arrangement.
> - **Facultative**: the party is free to act, for a cedant, this means they can choose whether to offer a risk, for a reinsurer it means they can choose to accept or reject it.
> - **Obligatory**: This freedom is removed. The parties must cede or accept the risks as defined by the treaty.

> [!info] What are the common combinations?
>- a. **Facultative/Facultative:** Both parties have freedom of action for each individual risk 
>- b. **Facultative/Obligatory:** The insurer chooses whether to cede a risk, but the reinsurer is legally **obliged** to accept it if offered
>- c. **Obligatory/Obligatory:** Both parties must cede and accept risks according to the terms of the formal treaty

> [!question] What **strategic advantage** does each type of arrangement provide?
>
>- **Speed and Efficiency:** Obligatory arrangements (types b and c) allow an insurer to issue large policies immediately without waiting for a reinsurer's approval, which prevents delays that could lose a sale 
>- **Flexibility and broader coverage:** Facultative arrangements provide flexibility, as they allow both the insurer and reinsurer to act freely regarding individual risks. Furthermore it allows for the coverage of specific high value policies that might fall outside the scope or retention limits of standard obligatory treaties.


> [!question] How does the cost of the reinsurance affect decisions about reinsurance arrangements?
> - Reinsurance comes at a cost, these **costs reduce the expected profit** for the cedant
>- The reinsurance adds **profit margins** to its risk premiums. 
>- The reinsurer may insist on more **stringent risk management** than the insurer would like e.g. stricter underwriting or claims processes
>- The reinsurance processes may result in a slower service offering to the policyholder (facultative, claims, delays)
> - The insurer will have increased **counterparty risk**, as it is exposed to the risk of the reinsurer failing to meet its obligations.
> - The direct writer must balance the **cost of reinsurance against the risk of loss** from adverse experience if less reinsurance were to be used.
> However, the **risk reduction** caused by reinsurance could **leverage up** the return or risk-adjusted return on capital

> [!example] Why don't insurer's just use mortality fluctuation reserve instead of costly reinsurance?
> - Cost of capital of the reserve being tied up
> - Limited protection (given finite resources), reinsurance can protect against more severe claims volatility for a smaller cost
> - Solvency risk for small companies, sudden surge in claims could exceed the practical reserves put in place
> - Declining risks, allows the cedant not to decline large risks (to manage volatility)

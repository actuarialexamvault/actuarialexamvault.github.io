 > [!question] How do insurer's determine the level of its retention limit using stochastic simulation?
 > Insurer's use stochastic simulation to determine retention limits by modelling the probability of future insolvency across thousands of potential scenarios. The primary goal is to identify a retention level that ensures the company stays solvent in a targeted percentage of outcomes, such as 99.5% of simulations. The process involves the following steps:
 > - ***Modelling claims***: insurers use a stochastic model to project the **number of claims** by assigning a probability distribution to the **mortality rate**, to model both the **mean mortality rate** and the **random fluctuations** around the mean.
 > - ***Model office:*** the model projects the company's **assets and liabilities** forward to see how different retention levels impact available capital
 > 
 > The retention level is highly sensitive to the **distribution of sum assureds** in the portfolio and the assumed **variance of mortality/morbidity rates**
 
> [!solvency] Stochastic simulation – reinsurance with fluctuations reserve
> In this approach, insurers aim to minimize the total cost of two components: 
> - the cost of obtaining reinsurance and 
> - the cost of financing a mortality fluctuations reserve.
>
>The process follows these steps:
> 1. ***Set a risk criterion***: such as a maximum 1% probability that net losses will exceed a specific amount e.g. 25m
> 2. ***Model total costs***: for different retention limits, the insurer stochastically models the expected net loss by varying the mortality rates. As the retention limit increases, the cost of reinsurance decreases, but the size of the required fluctuations reserve (and its associated financing cost) increases.
> 3. ***Calculate the financing cost***: The cost of the reserve ($M$) is calculated as $M \times (j - i)$, where $j$ is the required rate of return on the company's capital and $i$ is the actual investment return earned on the reserve's assets.
> 4. **Optimize**: The insurer identifies the specific retention limit where the combined cost of the reserve and reinsurance is lowest while still satisfying the ruin probability target.

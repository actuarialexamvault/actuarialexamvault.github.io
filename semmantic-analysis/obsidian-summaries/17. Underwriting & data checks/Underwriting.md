
> [!definition] What is underwriting?
> **Underwriting** is the process of assessing an insurance risk to determine if it is acceptable and, if so, setting the appropriate premium and terms.
>
Main types
> - **Medical underwriting** - evidence from proposal forms, doctors reports, medicals examinations and special tests to assess health
> - **Financial underwriting** - ensures sum assured is consistent with the applicants income to prevent fraud and over insurance
> - **Lifestyle underwriting** - assessment of hazardous hobbies, occupation and place of residence
> - **Claims underwriting** - verifying policy conditions were met and that initial disclosures were accurate

> [!steps] What are the benefits and drawbacks of underwriting?
>Benefits 
>- Anti-selection risk reduction
> - Fairness among healthy policyholders and those in substandard health
> - Fraud detection and protection
> - Aligns the pricing basis with the pool of lives
> 
> Drawbacks
> - Barriers to sales
> - High expenses
> - Complexity e.g. income protection

> [!caution] What are the potential outcomes for substandard lives?
>- Increase the premium
>- Decrease benefits
>- Exclusions
>- Defer the decision
>- Decline the proposal

>[!example] What are some alternatives to medical underwriting?
>- Simple underwriting questions
>- Waiting periods
>- Accident-only cover
>- Third party medicals
>- Claims underwriting 
>

> [!group] How is underwriting done on group business?
> **Free cover limits**: allow members to be accepted with no underwriting up to a certain benefit level. 
> **Actively at work requirement** when cover begins to reduce anti-selection risk
> **Compulsory cover** or minimum take-up rates for voluntary schemes
> **No financial underwriting** because salary is known.

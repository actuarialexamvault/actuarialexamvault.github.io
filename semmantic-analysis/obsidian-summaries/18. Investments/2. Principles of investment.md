
> [!solvency] What are the principles of investment?
> The principles of investment involve balancing the need for security with the desire for high returns.
> 
> The primary principles are:
> - **Matching**: Assets should be selected to match the **nature, term and currency** of the company's liabilities to minimize risk.
> - **Maximizing return**: Investments should be chosen to maximize the total return which includes both immediate income and capital gains
> - **Free assets**: The extent to which the company's investment strategy may move away from a matched position to seek higher returns will depend on the level of the company's free assets.

> [!caution] When is perfect matching not desirable and when might it be desirable?
>- Perfect matching is usually not desirable because it conflicts with the goal of maximizing investment returns.
>- Perfect matching is desirable under the following circumstances:
>	- No free assets to act as a cushion
>	- Risk minimization focus maximizing returns is not a goal
>	- Regulatory constraints
>	- Fixed and certain liabilities e.g. without-profit immediate annuities

> [!out-flow] How does the nature, term and currency of liabilities affect asset selection?
>- The liability outgo consists of:
> 	- Benefit payments
> 	- Expense outgo
> 	- minus: premium income
> - In any given year the liability outgo depends on the monetary amount of its constituents and the probability of it being received or paid out.
> 
> - The liabilities may be:
> 	- ***Guaranteed in money term***s: Match with fixed-interest bonds or cash
> 	- ***Guaranteed in terms of an index***: use index-linked bonds or potentially property investment (rental income) or equities that move in line with CPI.
> 		- Short-term index linked liabilities could be matched by fixed interest stocks
>
> 	- ***Discretionary***: backed by real assets like equity or property for higher bonuses
> 	- ***Investment-linked***: hold the exact assets the benefits are linked to.
>
>Term: 
> - The focus is on the [[../../Definitions/discounted mean term (duration)|discounted mean term (duration)]] of the liability. By matching the duration of assets to liabilities, the company ensures that if interest rates change, the values of both assets and liabilities move in line with each other. 
> - However insurance liabilities tend to be longer term that available assets in the market.
 >
 >Currency: 
 >- Assets should typically be held in the same currency as the liabilities.
>
>Uncertainty: 
>- Insurance liabilities are uncertain due to mortality/morbidity fluctuations
> 	- For a large porfolio, benefit outgo is more predictable
> 	- for a smaller portfolio, fluctuations may be more material

> [!caution] Issues with matching equity-index linked liabilities
>- The assets should be invested to match or replicate the equity index
>- Index replication requires holdings in the correct weights
>- Tracking the index requires monitoring and rebalancing
>- purchasing every stock in the index may be impractical
>- transaction costs from frequent trading may reduce returns below the required index-linked return 
>- buying and selling at the same time as other index trackers may move prices
>- inflows and outflows must be invest/disinvested in line with the index, this may be difficult for small cashflows
>- the insurer may use specialist index-tracking strategies
>- proxy portfolios or quantitative replication techniques may be used
>- these would require expertise that a company might not have

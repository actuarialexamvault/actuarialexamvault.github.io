
> [!money-tree] How do free assets affect the investment strategy of an insurance company?
> Free assets act as a financial cushion that allows insurers to move away from a strictly "matched" investment position to seek higher returns. 
> - ***Maximizing returns***: Companies can invest in riskier assets to improve overall returns, which can lead to higher bonuses for with-profits policyholders or higher dividends for shareholders. 
> - ***Solvency buffer***: Free assets reduce the probability of insolvency by absorbing potential falls in the market value of assets backing guaranteed benefits
> - ***Investment freedom***: the greater the level of free assets the more freedom to mismatch the nature, term, and currency of liabilities, provided the risks remain within the company's total financial resources.

> [!trending-down] Why is mismatching investment-linked liabilities uncommon?
> Mismatching investment unit-linked assets and liabilities is uncommon for the following reasons:
> - ***Regulatory constraints***: In many markets, life insurance companies are legally required to invest in the exact assets used to determine the policyholder benefits.
> - ***Shareholder investment risk***: any profit or loss resulting from a mismatch belongs to the owner of the business, not the shareholders. This exposes shareholders to direct investment risk that they may not want to bear.
> - ***Risk elimination***: By investing in the same assets used to determine the benefits, the insurer avoids matching problems entirely because assets and liabilities move perfectly in tandem.
> - ***Administrative simplicity***: It is simpler to manage the fund where the assets directly replicate the unit linked liabilities.


> [!definition] What is expense control in risk management?
> Expense control is a key strategy for managing risk, particularly the risk that actual expenses exceed those assumed during pricing. Therefore it seeks to **contain** expenses and commissions within the **expense loadings** built into the company's **premiums and charging rates**. 

>[!max] How do insurers control expenses?
>- The first thing the insurer does is to **monitor** the expenses closely against policy loadings
>- The insurer needs to be monitoring the different **categories of expenses** separately (initial, renewal and investment)
>- By reducing the **current cost base** of the insurer to be within the loadings, the insurer ensures that the income from loadings is able to pay for expense outgo to avoid making **expense losses** which could result in insolvency in the long run.
>- Comparisons will also be made with the **expense ratios** of **competitors** where possible. The ratio of **actual expenses over premiums** is likely to be used to compare to competitors. To keep the insurers premiums competitive so that it is able to keep sales volumes at an adequate level. 

> [!example] What are some of the possible ways that an insurer might try to keep its expenses within its policy loadings?
>- ***Spreading fixed costs through volume of business***:
>	- High sales of profitable business effectively lower the per-policy expense because a large portion of costs are fixed overheads
>	- Changes in product design or sales methods that increase profitability and marketability
>	- Impose targets and budgetary constraints within which individual departments are required to manage
>- ***Improving efficiency***: 
>	- Automation and computerisation of tasks, so that **less manpower** is required.
>	- **Streamlining underwriting procedures** if it can be done without compromising risks.
>	- Doing tasks **in-house** instead of using external consultants
>	- Using **cheaper distribution channels** such as the internet
>	- Selling **simpler products** so that sales and administration costs are low
>- **Staff**:
>	- Maintain active control of staffing levels (sales and underwriting), as to reflect the volume of business and the amount of work being achieved. 
>	- Do not use overqualified staff or overpay for the work they are required to do
>	- Ensure that **cost-of-living and grade salary increases** are not excessive but consistent with the price needed to retain the necessary **quality** of staff.
>- **Increase the loadings in the premium** if the competitive position allows.

> [!steps] How should an insurance company control commissions?
> Commissions should be controlled by monitoring at the level of:
> - Product line
> - Distribution channel
> - The specific sales person or broker
>
>Commission rates usually start rising from the bottom up, which start at the sales person or broker, up to the distribution channel and finally we see commission increasing for the entire product line.

> [!compare] How does an insurance company manage mismatching issue that can arise from a the mismatch in incidence and amount of expenses and charges or loadings?
> To reduce the mismatch between actual expenses and policy charges or loadings, the insurer could:
> - Restrict or encourage certain product lines 
> 	- directly by structural change
> 	- indirectly via renumeration arrangements, level of support given, or through marketing
> - Change distribution channels e.g. to lower initial commission channels
>- Reprice contracts or change product design, increasing the minimum premium if possible which will reduce the risk of lower than expected policy size affecting the balance of cross-subsidies. 

| Expense Category | Typical Matching Loading or Policy Charge                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| :--------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Initial**      | **initial acquisition charges**, which are typically a **percentage of the first year's premium** for commissions and a **flat per-policy cost** (sometimes combined with a **percentage of the benefit size**) for overheads, underwriting and administration allowing cross subsidy. |
| **Renewal**      | **flat per-policy loading** (or adjusted for inflation) for ongoing maintenance and/or a **percentage of regular premiums** to cover recurring expenses like renewal commissions.                                                                                                      |
| **Investment**   | **deduction from the investment return**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

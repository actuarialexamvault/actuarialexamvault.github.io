
> [!solvency] What is the primary concern in the management of new business?
> The insurer needs to make sure it has **sufficient capital** and **administrative resources** to allow the writing of new business. 
> In extreme cases, the actuary may recommend that directors withdraw from selling products that are more capital intensive or for which there are no administrative resources available.
> On a regular basis, the insurer needs to monitor the mismatch between the actual volume and mix of new business sold and that assumed in pricing.

> [!floor] How does the business monitor and control new business?
> The following items need to be monitored by product line and distribution channel:
> - Number of contracts
> - Amount of premium
>
>It is also important to monitor for new business how it affects the company's capital position, for which it is important to consider:
> - premium frequency
> - policy charges/loadings
> - new businesss strain:
> 	- acquisition expenses incurred
> 	- capital requirements (reserves + solvency margin)
> - the mismatch between income and outgo (expenses vs loadings and charges) which has an impact in determining new business strain.

> [!solvency] How does a company manage new business valuation strain?
> - Through product design: A major factor in product design is the amount of guarantees given. The greater the level of guarantee, the more conservative the valuation basis has to be, and the greater the valuation strain. Therefore, if the company wants to reduce new business valuation strain it should move to product designs that offer less guarantees such as:
> 	- Unit-linked designs
> 	- Less onerous guarantees and options
> 	- Reviewable premiums or charges

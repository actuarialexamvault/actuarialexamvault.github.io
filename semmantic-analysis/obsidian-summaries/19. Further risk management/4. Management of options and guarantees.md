
> [!caution] How should insurers manage the risk of options and guarantees?
> - The insurer should monitor the charges and loadings included for options in the premiums against the actual costs being experienced. If there are losses with respect to the pricing of the option, the insurer would then consider increasing charges for the option.
> - The insurer must also weigh the benefit against the cost of offering the option. 
> 	- The potential benefit is the increased marketability of the product because the option is attractive and satisfies a customer need which would increase new business volumes. 
> 	- The cost is the actual net loss of the option or the potential for presenting unacceptable risk of losses in future. 
> - The time lag between the removal of an option and the impact on experience emerging must also be kept in mind. removing the option from new policies will only decrease the actual option cost at future dates only when the exercise dates of all options in force have passed.
> - It is easier to amend the terms of new business written and it may be possible to reduce the impact of options under existing business by a strict interpretation of policy wording, subject to treating customers fairly.
> - Future looses for options in force may be dealt with by:
> 	- setting up appropriate (prudent) reserves 
> 	- hedging the options by purchasing appropriate financial derivatives
> 	- offering to buy back the option from policyholders (although this could encourage everyone to keep their option)

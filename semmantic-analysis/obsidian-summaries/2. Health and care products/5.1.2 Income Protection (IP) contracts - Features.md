
> [!ABSTRACT] General principles for Policy Conditions
> Often seen as the most complex part of the product because the must cover procedure to be followed under various scenarios (lapse, claim, recovery, partial recovery, retirement, death).
> - ***Clarity***: conditions must clearly specify insured and uninsured events in words the policyholder understands to avoid the risk of courts or regulators interpreting ambiguities in favour of the claimant.
> - ***Intent***: must reflect the true intentions of the office
> - Give some cushion (in the form of exclusions) against adverse events over which the office has no control.
> - ***Business support***: Simple and unambiguous conditions assist the sales, underwriting and claims processes.

> [!family] Benefits
> Benefits are usually linked to current salary:
> - ***Replacement ratios and limits***: To incentivice return to work, benefits are subject to limits, often expressed as percentage of pre-disability salary (e.g. 75%). 
> 
> - ***Escalation***: Benefits may be level or include fixed escalation (e.g. 5% p.a.) or move in line with a price or earnings index. Benefits may increase at different rates in and out of claim, though this can lead to complex product design.

> [!lapse] Waiver of premium 
> A condition that premiums are waived during periods when benefits are payable. 

> [!trending-down] Rehabilitation/Proportionate benefits
> A condition to pay a **reduced benefit** if a claimant returns to work on a part-time basis or lower-paid-role to motivate transition back to work while providing support. Some products attempt to assist with **recuperation** needs.

^2c5f8d

> [!trending-up] Over-insurance
> Where a claimant might receive more income while sick than when working will arise if the replacement ratio is too high, which may discourage return to work. Over-insurance can arise from:
> 	- ***over-insurance from outset*** (addressed by quality training, maximum benefit formulas, policy conditions)
> 	- ***subsequent over-insurance***, through salary not keeping up with benefits (regular reviews, policy conditions)
> 	- ***a reduction in the tax*** levied on IP claims (regular reviews)
> 	- ***multiple policies*** or other ***non-disclosed sources of income*** while sick (clear policy wording, deducts for any other income/benefit)

> [!time] Benefit timing
> These terms define **when** benefits are payable:
>- ***[[Waiting Period]]:*** The primary aim is to manage anti-selection, (not as effective as underwriting). 
>- ***[[Deferred Period]]***: e.g. 4, 13, 26 and 52 weeks. Usually leads to lower premiums:
>	- Integrates with employer/state sickness benefits
>	- reduce the cost of claims (and hence premiums)
>	- reduce administration costs (and hence premiums)
>	- meet customer needs (flexibility)
>- ***Linked-claims period***: Waives the deferred period if sickness recurs within a specified period after returning to work. Designed to encourage return to work. 
>- ***Expiry age or term***: The expiry age is the age at which benefits cease and is often the same as the expected retirement age. Some cases such as mortgage business, are written for a fixed term.

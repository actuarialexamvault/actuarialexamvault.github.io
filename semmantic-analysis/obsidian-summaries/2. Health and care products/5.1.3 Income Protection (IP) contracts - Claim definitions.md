
> [!ABSTRACT] Incapacity
> A valid claim is triggered by **incapacity**, which is defined as the inability of the insured individual to work due to illness or accident. Defining incapacity requires a balance between meeting the needs of genuine claimants and maintaining a controllable insurance risk.

> [!CAUTION] Occupational Definitions
> The four primary levels of occupational definitions:
> - ***Own occupation***: The inability to perform the specific job the insured held at the time of disability.
> - ***Suited/reasonable occupation***: The inability to perform one's own occupation and any other occupation for which the insured is suited by education, status or training.
> - ***Initial own occupation***: A hybrid definition where the "own occupation" criteria applied for an initial period (e.g. first-two years), followed by an "*any occupation*" definition thereafter.
> - ***Any occupation***: The most restrictive definition, requiring the insured to be unable to perform **any work** whatsoever.
> 
> The choice of definition significantly impacts **pricing** and **risk** of claims. The "own occupation" definition typically results int he highest expected claim costs, while "any occupation" is the lowest. 

> [!danger] Alternative Incapacity definitions
>- ***Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)***: These assess the ability to perform a number of **basic everyday tasks**, typically including feeding, dressing, washing, toileting, mobility and transferring.
>- ***Personal Capability Assessment (PCA)***: These assess the ability to perform specific **physical and mental skills necessary for work**, such as the ability to rise from a chair, stand for ten minutes, walk 200 metres or use a keyboard.
>- ***Other Tests***: Insurers may also use **Functional Assessment Tests (FATs)** or **Activities of Daily Working (ADWs)** to provide more objective, work-focused criteria related to the claimant's **occupation class** focussing on functional skills such as dexterity, mobility, and communication.


> [!INFO] Administrative and Legal considerations
>- Claim definitions must be simple and unambiguous to assist the sales, underwriting and claims processes. If definitions are unclear, they run the risk of being interpreted by courts or regulators in favour of the claimant during disputes. 

> [!EXAMPLE] Further Policy conditions
>- ***Early notification of sickness***: IP policies may require the insured to notify the insurer early into a sickness (e.g. after one month), even if the deferred period is much longer to allow for **early rehabilitation intervention**.
>- ***Changes in Occupation***: IP policies may require the policyholder to inform the office if the change their occupation to allow the insurer to modify the premium if the new job carried a **different risk class** 
>	- although recent trend is moving away from requiring this specific notification preferring to base claim assessments on where the claimant would be able to carry out the former occupation.
>- ***Changes in residence or location***: Policy conditions often require policyholders to advice the insurer of a change in residence for longer than a limited period, especially if moving outside of specified territories. Because a change in location represents a significant change in risk factors including access to quality healthcare and exposure to different diseases. 
>- ***Evidence of Ongoing disability***: In IP the insurer will put procedures in place for monitoring the ongoing claim, which requires the policyholder to participate in periodic visits and provide **evidence of continued incapacity**.


> [!summary] Product variations
Variations of an IP product that might exist.
>- Guaranteed and reviewable premium rates
>- Guaranteed insurability options (GIOs)
>- No claims discounts

> [!tldr] Guaranteed and reviewable premium rates
>- IP premium designs exist at two extremes: A **full guarantee** of future premiums or **no guarantee** whatsoever
>- A frequent "middle ground" is to **guarantee the premium amount for an initial period**, such as the first 5 years, with the insurer gaining the freedom to review and change rates thereafter
>- **Reviewable premium** structure:
>	- Changes are made based on the experience of the entire portfolio of policies, rather than the claims history of the individual policyholder.
>	- Reviewable approaches are used because IP risks are influenced by **unforeseen economic and social factors**, such as unemployment trends and long-term changes in the workplace or medical science
>	- Insurers with reviewable policies face the risk of **selective withdrawal**, where healthy individuals leave the portfolio following a premium increase, leaving the pool with sub-standard lives who are more likely to claim.
>
>- **Guaranteed premiums** 
>	- Generally more expenses because they require a **substantial contingency loading** to protect the insurer against the high uncertainty of future claims experience.

> [!INFO] Guaranteed Insurability Options (GIOs)
>-  [[Guaranteed Insurability Options (GIOs)]] are less common in IP than in life insurance** because the primary function of IP is salary replacement, though some new versions allow cover to fluctuate annually in line with income.


> [!money-tree] No claims discounts 
>- Some IP contracts feature a no claims discount, which **offers a reduced premium** if the policyholder has not made an IP claim
>- This variation serves as a method of **rating the policy by the policyholder's actual experience**, which can help compensate for any limited medical underwriting performed at the policy's inception.
> 

> [!SUMMARY] Structural Basis
> Most IP is written on a **without-profit basis**, other structures can be found 
>- ***With-profits IP***: The include a savings element where annual bonuses are declared, building up to provide a benefit upon death or the expiry of the policy term. 
>- ***Unit linked IP***: Morbidity charges are deducted from a unit fund. The investment risk is borne by the policyholder, and any remaining units are paid as a maturity benefit at the end of the term.


> [!skull] Morbidity
> The primary risk to an insurer selling UP contracts is uncertainty regarding **sickness transfer probabilities** specifically the **claim inception rates** (healthy to sick) and **claim termination rate**s (sick to healthy or dead).
> - **Parameter risk**: The risk that the parameters used in the insurer's model do not adequately reflect the future experience of the specific class of lives insured. 
> - **Definition leniency**: The more lenient the policy's definition of incapacity, the higher the risk. Boarder definitions increase **both** the expected cost of claims and the uncertainty of those estimates.
> - **Anti-selection**: is a significant risk for individual contracts, where persons who know they are ill or likely to claim seek coverage. In group contracts, this risk is generally lower but can manifest if an employer and workforce collaborate to use the policy as a **de facto redundancy scheme**.
> - **Moral hazard**: Insured individuals have less financial incentive to return to work than those without insurance, which can lead to delayed recoveries or exaggerated claims.
> - **Longevity risk**: if claimants live longer than expected, (claim termination probabilities are lower than assumed) then benefits are paid for longer durations increasing the cost of claims.

> [!trending-down] Investment risk
> There is a risk that investment returns on the assets backing the business will be lower than assumed in the pricing basis.
>  

> [!Expense risk]
> Profits may be reduced by:
> - ***Higher than expected inflation*** 
> - ***Poor business volumes*** 
> - ***Poor operational management of expenses***

> [!lapse] [[Withdrawal (Persistency) risk]]
> - ***Negative asset share***: If a policy lapses early when the asset share is negative, the insurer suffers a permanent financial loss.
> - ***Selective withdrawals***: Healthy lives are more likely to lapse, leaving the insurer with a **sub-standard pool of risks** with higher average morbidity. 

> [!caution] Data Accuracy
>  **Policy Data Risk:** If the insurer fails to maintain accurate and complete records, actuarial valuations may be incorrect, potentially leading to **insolvency or incorrect business decisions**. This is particularly challenging for IP due to the **complexity** of recording regular payments and detailed claim information

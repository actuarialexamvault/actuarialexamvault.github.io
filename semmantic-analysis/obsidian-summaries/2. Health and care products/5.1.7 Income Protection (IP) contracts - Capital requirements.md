> [!summary] Capital requirements for Income Protection (IP) contracts
> The following factors affect capital requirements for IP contracts:
>
> - **Design of the Contract** (High): Unit-linked designs are generally more capital-efficient than non-linked designs. Contracts featuring reviewable charges or reviewable premiums are more efficient than conventional guaranteed products.
> - **Solvency Capital Requirement** (High): Supervisory authorities may require additional capital to account for the fact that sickness rates are significantly more unpredictable than death rates. IP business often requires higher prudent margins than life contracts for this reason.
> - **Initial Expenses** (High): IP business involves heavy acquisition costs, specifically due to the complexity of medical underwriting and the potential for higher commissions paid to intermediaries.
> - **Relationship Between Bases** (Critical): The amount of capital required depends heavily on the extent of the difference between the pricing basis and the supervisory reserving basis.
> - **Premium Frequency** (High): While individual IP policies are typically paid by regular premium, the availability of single premium contracts would fundamentally alter capital needs.

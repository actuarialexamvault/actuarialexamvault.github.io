
> [!info] Product structures
>CI cover is typically provided in three forms
>- **Stand-alone:** The sum assured is paid only upon diagnosis, with no benefit paid on death. 
>- **Rider:** CI is provided as an additional feature to a [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/1. Life insurance contracts|life policy]]
>- **Accelerated Benefit:** The sum assured is paid on the earlier of diagnosis or death.

>[!group] Group CI products
> The group version is attractive to those employees and individuals for whom [[Income Protection Insurance]] is not available, including some **blue-collar** occupations, and is of particular value when **TPD** cover is included.

> [!family] Benefits 
> A critical illness product may offered with a benefit payable in any combination of the following ways:
>- upon **diagnosis** of a critical illness e.g. Cancer
>- upon the happening of a **critical illness event** e.g. a heart attack
>- on reaching a **defined degree of impairment** e.g. losing the ability to walk
>- on undergoing a **surgical procedure**. e.g. major organ transplant

> [!steps] Tiered benefits
> A variation of standard CI where the payout is linked to the **objective** severity of the disease. 
> - ***Comprehensiveness***: They provide cover for less severe stages of a disease that would not trigger a payment under a standard CI contract.
> - ***Multiple claims***: The policy does not terminate on first claim, additional payments are made from the remaining balance of the sum assured. Enhances policyholder satisfaction and encourages them to keep the policy in force.
> - ***Pricing and design complexity***: difficulty creating objective severity definitions and deriving transition intensities. Complexity may also reduce appeal to financial advisors or create potential for claim disputes.
> - ***Market differentiation***: allow insurers to differentiate themselves from competitors and make direct price comparisons more difficult.
> - ***Underwriting and claims management***: Stringent under underwriting for pre-existing conditions. Higher volume of forms and increase pressure to **upgrade** to a higher benefit level.
 
> [!check] Complimentary cover
>- ***Terminal illness***: Added to cover any condition that significantly reduces life expectancy (e.g. to less than 12 months)
>- ***Children's rider benefit***: provides restricted cover for the policyholders children, typically until they reach 18.
>- **Total and Permanent Disability (TPD**): included to cover severe, permanent disability that may not be explicityly named in the list of diseaseses but is lifestyle-threatening. 

> [!Note] Guarantees and reviewability of premiums and benefits
>- ***Competitive pressures***: pushed companies to offer guarantees on both benefits (that claim definitions will not change) and premiums.
>- ***Better estimation*** of CI risks leads to insurers progressively charging an appropriate price for guarantees.
>- ***Predicting increases***: For reviewable contracts, it is difficult to predict future premium increases because incidence trends for different medical conditions move in different directions.
>- ***Impact on Reserving and capital***: Guaranteed premiums may require capital injections to fund reserves for worsening claims experience.
>- ***Impact of medical advances***
>	- ***Windfall claims***: Medical advances may turn a previously "critical" surgery into a minor, low risk procedure. If the benefit criteria is guaranteed, the insurer is forced to pay the full sum assured for that condition. 
>	- ***Early diagnosis***: Improved screening may detect diseases earlier, triggering "guaranteed" payouts much sooner than insurers originally anticipated.
>- ***New diseases***: insurers often include new diseases at little or no extra cost but new additions must be properly costed.
>-  ***[[Guaranteed Insurability Options (GIOs)]]***: Allow a policyholder to increase their sum insured without providing further medical evidence, triggered by specific life events or specific policy anniversaroes.


> [!skull] Morbidity risk
> The primary risk to an insurer issuing **Critical Illness (CI)** products is the uncertainty regarding incidence rates for the specified conditions
> - **Parameter risk**: Because these contracts are relatively new to many markets, historical experience data is often limited, unreliable, or non-existent, leading to significant **parameter risk**.
> - **Medical and Technological Advances:** Improved screening and medical technology can lead to **earlier diagnosis** of conditions like cancer, increasing claim frequency sooner than originally priced. Furthermore, advances may turn a previously "critical" surgery into a minor procedure (e.g., angioplasty), resulting in **[[../../Definitions/Windfall claims|windfall claims]].** 
>
>- **Data Heterogeneity:** Because different companies cover different sets of illnesses and use varying medical definitions, industry-wide data is often **heterogeneous** and can be misleading when used as a basis for a specific insurer’s assumptions.
>
>- **Anti-selection and Selective Withdrawals:** There is a significant risk of **anti-selection**, where individuals who suspect they are at high risk seek out cover. Additionally, **selective withdrawals** occur when healthy lives lapse their policies, leaving the insurer with a pool of higher-risk individuals.
>- **Guarantees:** Competitive pressures have forced many insurers to offer **guaranteed premiums and benefit definitions**. If claims experience worsens or medical advances increase diagnosis rates, the insurer may be legally required to pay claims based on outdated definitions and must fund **significant reserve enhancements** from shareholder capital or with-profits funds.
>- **Tiered Benefit Complexity:** Offering **tiered benefits** based on the severity of a condition introduces additional risks, including the difficulty of finding statistics for **transition intensities** between severity levels and the potential for **claims disputes** regarding the exact degree of impairment.

> [!caution] Other risks
>- **Claims Management and Underwriting:** The complexity of medical definitions requires high levels of expertise in **claims underwriting**. Insurers face pressure from policyholders or doctors to "upgrade" claims to higher severity levels, and poor decisions can lead to **reputational damage or bad press**.

> [!receipt] Expense risk
>- Stand-alone CI contracts carry **expense risk**, particularly if business volumes are lower than expected to cover fixed overheads. 

> [!trending-down] Investment risk
>- While **investment risk** is generally minimal because reserves for pure protection policies are small, it remains a factor if assets and liabilities are mismatched

> [!danger] Difficulties with tiered benefits
> - Data scarcity and relevance
> - Claim definitions for the different severity levels for each of the conditions covered
>  - Multistate modelling and spurious accuracy
>  - Dependency of incidence rates between severity levels
>  - Medical advances make it difficult to predict future experience
> - Difficulty calculating the survival factor after a CI event especially for accelerated benefits because there is a timing overlap between CI and death benefits
> - Underwriting and claims guidelines complexity
> - Staff training
> - Administration system complexity
> - Claim disputes and reputational risk
>

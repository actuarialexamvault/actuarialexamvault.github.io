
> [!abstract] Product types
>- ***Pre-funded plans***: purchased by healthy individuals to protect against the future risk of disability
>- ***Immediate needs plans***: purchased by individuals who already need care. 

> [!example] Product structures
>- ***Stand-alone plans***: These are protection-focused, pre-funded solutions.
>- ***Integrated riders***: LTCI benefits can be added to other policies to create a comprehensive package.
>	- ***With Critical illness (CI)***: usually structured so that the definition of Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) switches to **"loss of independent existence"** (based on Activities of Daily Living) after retirement
>	- **With Whole of Life Insurance:** This contract **accelerates a fixed percentage** of the death benefit (e.g., 2% per month) if the policyholder satisfies long-term care criteria.
>	- **With Income Protection (IP):** The cover is extended beyond normal retirement age, at which point the definition of disability switches from being **occupation-related** to **activity-related** (ADLs)

> [!family] Product bases
> Variations in LTCI often center on how benefits are defined and how the policy is funded:
>
>
>
>- **Unit-Linked LTCI:** These act primarily as **investment/savings vehicles** where long-term care "risk charges" are deducted from the unit fund. Policyholders may have options regarding **"fund protection,"** deciding whether the unit fund should be exhausted to pay for care or if the initial investment should be protected for beneficiaries.
>
>- **Premium Variations:** Premiums can be **guaranteed at outset or reviewable** based on the insurer's experience. Funding methods include single premiums, regular premiums (which may stop at a certain age or during disability), or **retrospective payments** via equity release from the sale of a home.
>
>- **Tiered/Severity-Based Payouts:** Many plans offer different benefit levels (e.g., **50% payout** for failing two ADLs and **100%** for failing three or more).
>
>- **Immediate Needs Variations:** These can include **death benefits** such as a minimum payment period or the return of part of the single premium if the insured dies early.
>
>- **Pension Solutions:** These are **"pseudo-pension annuity"** designs where a conventional annuity is augmented by a rider that increases income if an impairment trigger is met in retirement.
>
>- **Supportive Options:** Many variations include non-financial benefits like **assistive devices** (stair lifts, grab rails), **personal alarms**, and access to **independent care advice** or a care manager

^2b80d3
> [!receipt] Cash vs. Indemnity Benefits
>- **Cash benefits** provide a fixed income or lump sum used flexibly by the policyholder.
>- **Indemnity benefits** pay for the **actual cost of treatment or care**, often paid directly to the service provider, though these may be subject to a "cap" or cash limit.

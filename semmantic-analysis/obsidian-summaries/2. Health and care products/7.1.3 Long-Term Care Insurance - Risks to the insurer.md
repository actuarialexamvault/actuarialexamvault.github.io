
> [!skull] Morbidity risk
>- ***Transfer probabilities***: The primary risk relates to the sickness transfer probabilities in the multistate model, Specifically claim inception probabilities and rates of transition between claim states (e.g. from home care to residential or nursing care). 
>- ***Direction of transition***: Unlike IP, LTCI models generally assume that there is no recovery transition (i.e. deteriorating health and higher claims costs)
>- ***Data scarcity***: because LTCI is a relatively new product with low demand in many markets, there is a shortage of reliable historical data to base estimates for transfer probabilities.
>- ***Geographic data incompatibility***: data from established markets may be inappropriate for other counties due to differences in demographics, social attitudes toward self-help and policy wordings.
>- ***Anti-selection***: individuals who suspect they have a higher probability of needing care will be more inclined to purchase the product.
>- ***Moral hazard***: Even with objective triggers like [[Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)|ADLs]], claims assessments are subject to value judgements, the insurer is at risk of exploitative claims from policyholders who may not strictly meet the disability definition but require assistance.
>- ***Selective withdrawals***: Healthy policyholders are more likely to lapse their policies, leaving the insurer with a substandard pool of high-risk lives.

> [!trending-down] Investment risk
>- ***Investment risk***: because LTCI is a pure protection policy that can span many decades, significant reserves can build up, exposing the insurer to substantial investment risk over a long period.
>- ***Inflation of care costs***: for products that provide indemnity benefits (covering actual care costs), the insurer faces the risk that the inflation of nursing and residential care-home fees.

> [!receipt] Expense risk
> Actual marginal and fixed costs of administering these complex, long-term contracts might be higher than the expense premium loadings. 

>[!caution] External and operational risks
>- ***Reputational and marketing risk***: Policyholders often expect the contract to cover the entire cost of care, but some products provide fixed cash benefits that may prove inadequate to meet actual future costs. Failure to meet these expectations can damage the reputation of the insure and lead to loss of future sales.
>- ***Political sensitivity***: LTCI is a politically sensitive issue, future changes in State attitudes and benefits (e.g. reduction in level of free nursing care) could cause raise costs if the insurer provides indemnity based care.
>- ***Regulatory interpretation***: There is a risk that regulators may interpret the "promise of care" more broadly than the insurer intended, forcing enhancements to benefits beyond what was priced.

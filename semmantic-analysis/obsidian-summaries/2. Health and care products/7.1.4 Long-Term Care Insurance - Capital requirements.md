> [!summary] Capital requirements for Long-Term Care Insurance (LTCI)
> Capital requirements for LTCI are determined by initial capital strain from initial cash outflows, prudent reserves, and required solvency margins:
>
> - **Contract Design and Guarantees** (Significant): Guarantees on premiums or benefits increase the risk profile, necessitating increased prudence and larger reserves. Unit-linked designs can be more capital-efficient if they allow the insurer to recoup expenses before building the unit fund.
> - **Reserving vs. Pricing Basis** (Significant): Capital is required at the outset if the supervisory reserving basis is stronger than the pricing basis. This is particularly relevant for LTCI due to the high level of uncertainty in future care costs and claim inception rates.
> - **Initial Expenses** (High Initial Strain): LTCI typically involves complex medical underwriting, marketing, and setup costs. When these initial expenses exceed the first premium, it creates a new business strain that must be financed by capital.
> - **Premium Frequency** (Moderate to High): Regular premium contracts require more initial capital than single premium contracts because the initial outgo often exceeds the first premium. Immediate needs plans (single premium) focus more on managing survival duration risk.
> - **Solvency Capital Requirements** (Very High): Regulatory authorities require a minimum solvency margin or cushion in addition to mathematical reserves. This required margin ties up free assets and increases the total finance needed to write the business.
> - **Nature of the Contingency** (Variable): Unlike life insurance where payment is certain, LTCI benefits are not certain to be paid (the policyholder may die first). However, because claims can last for the remaining lifetime once they begin, significant reserves must be built up in advance.

> [!key] Similarity to Endowments
> Capital requirements for LTCI are often comparable to endowment or whole life assurance because of the long-term pattern of paying regular premiums in advance for a benefit that has no fixed latest payment time. Unlike endowment or whole life assurance where payment is certain, LTCI benefits are not certain to be paid (the policyholder may die first). However, because claims can last for the remaining lifetime once they begin, significant reserves must be built up in advance.

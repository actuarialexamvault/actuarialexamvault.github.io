
> [!important] What are the basic data requirements for monitoring to be meaningful?
> 
> - **Consistency and stability**:  The basic requirement is that there is a **reasonable** **volume** of **stable** and **consistent** data, from which future experience and trends can be deduced. Data should be in a similar format, same source and grouped according to the same criteria
> - **Homogenous risk groups**: The problem is that we want to know why a change in some observed variable happened (e.g. mortality). Grouping heterogeneous lives into single cells for analysis means that we won't be able to tell whether a change in the mortality is due to a change in the mix of business.
> - **Credibility**: must balance the desire for homogenous groups against the need for statistical credibility. If the data cells are too small the results may be unreliable due to random fluctuations. 
> - **Time interval**: the period over which data is collected varies by the type of investigation
> 	- Recent vs old data: there is a conflict between wanting a large volume of data (which requires a longer interval) and wanting to investigate current experience (which requires a short interval).
> 	- Expense investigations are typically for 12 months to avoid seasonal distortions
> 	- Mortality investigations often use a 3 to 4 year span.

>[!question] What is the role of "Big data" in monitoring?
>**Benefits**
>- refine **risk classification**. 
>- **accurate individual rating** 
>	- For example, life insurance may be rated on factors that directly affect mortality such as diet and exercise.
>- **identify "preferred risks"** 
>- **influence customer behaviours** e.g. advice on healthy eating
>
**Challenges**
>- subject to **legislative restrictions** on personal information
>- risk of **reputational damage** from privacy violations
>- The data collected may be inaccurate, incomplete or irrelevant, there is also model risk 
>- The expense of collecting and analysing the data may outweigh the benefits of the extra information provided.

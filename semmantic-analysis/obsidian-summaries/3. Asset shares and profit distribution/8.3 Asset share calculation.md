
> [!math] The recursive formula
>Asset shares are typically calculated recursively on a year to year basis, starting from an initial value of zero at policy inception. Because asset shares are expressed per policy in force, the calculation must account for the proportion of policyholders who survive each year.
>
>The calculation identifies all actual income and expenditure associated with the contract over its lifetime to date.
>
>The recursive formula for an **individual policy** is: $$AS_{t+1} = \frac{(AS_t + P - E)(1 + i) - q'_{x+t} \cdot S}{1 - q'_{x+t}}$$
>Where:
>- **$AS_t$**: Asset share at the start of the year.
>- **$P$**: Premium paid.
>- **$E$**: Per-policy expenses.
>- **$i$**: Actual investment return achieved during the year.
>- **$S$**: Death benefit sum assured.
>- **$q'_{x+t}$**: Actual mortality rate experienced during the year.
>
>The formula assumes that **claims are paid at the end of the year**. Therefore the cost of the death strain is not rolled up with interest.

> [!info] Death strain at risk
> 
> The _individual_ asset share calculation is designed to deduct the **"death benefit in excess of the asset share"** (the death strain) rather than the full benefit. This is demonstrated by rearranging the formula into the following form: $$AS_{t+1} = (AS_t + P - E)(1+i) - q'_{x+t}(S - AS_{t+1})$$
Here **$(S - AS_{t+1})$** represents the **death strain at risk**, which is the actual amount the company "loses" when a policyholder dies, relative to the funds already accumulated for that policy.

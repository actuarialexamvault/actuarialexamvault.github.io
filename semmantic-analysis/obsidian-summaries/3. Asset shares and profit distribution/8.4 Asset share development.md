
> [!trending-down] Initial phase: [[New Business Strain]]
> For most regular premium contracts, the asset share is negative in the first few months or years. This is because the initial expenses often significantly exceed the first premium received. 

> [!trending-up] Growth phase: Compounding and balance of factors
> After the initial phase, the asset share's development is dictated by the interplay of the three major components. 
> - ***Investment return***: Actual earned rates of return (including both income and capital appreciation) have a **compounding effect** that drives the asset share upward.
> - ***Renewal expenses***: Ongoing administration and renewal commissions act as a steady deduction
> - ***Cost of cover***: The deduction for providing the death benefit (the "death strain at risk") generally **increases year by year** as the policyholder ages.
>
If investment income outweighs the cost of cover and renewal expenses, the asset share will grow in a **compound fashion**. However, if investment yields are low or expenses and claim costs are high, the growth may take a **convex shape**, where the asset share increases but at a gradually reducing rate

> [!trajectories] Product-specific trajectories
> The shape of asset share development varies significantly depending on the contract type:
> 
> **1. Endowment Assurances**
> 
> ![[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Diagrams/Pasted image 20260126070858.png]]
> The asset share for an [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/1.1 Endowment assurances|endowment assurance]] typically follows a curve that starts negative due to initial expenses, crosses zero, and then compounds upwards. By the maturity date, the asset share is expected to exceed the guaranteed sum insured, with the difference representing the policy's accumulated profit.
> 
> **2. Term Assurances**
> 
> ![[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Diagrams/Pasted image 20260126071107.png]]
>For a [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/2.2 Term assurances|temporary (term) assurance]], the asset share trajectory is different. It rises initially when level premiums are higher than the early cost of cover. As the age-related cost of life cover increases and exceeds the premium, the asset share decreases, ending at a small positive value that represents the company's profit.
>
> **3. Impact of Options and deductions**
> The development of the asset share is also influenced by various policy options and management actions. The chart below illustrates these impacts on a base asset share curve:
>
>- **Surrender Profits:** When policyholders withdraw with a [[Surrender Value]] lower than their asset share, the per-policy asset share of remaining policies increases.
  >  
>- **Capital Costs:** A deduction made at early durations to compensate the company for the initial capital strain.
>
>- **Discretionary Deductions:** Ongoing reductions for transfers to shareholders, taxes, and contributions to the undistributed surplus.

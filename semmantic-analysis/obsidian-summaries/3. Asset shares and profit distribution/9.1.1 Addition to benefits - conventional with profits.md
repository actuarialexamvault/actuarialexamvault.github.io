
> [!in-flow] Addition to benefits
> The additions to benefits method distributes profits in direct relation to current benefits. 

> [!trending-up] Regular reversionary bonuses
>- Usually declared **annually** 
>-  ***Guaranteed***: Once a reversionary bonus is declared, it becomes a [[Guarantee]].
>-  ***Smoothed***: The insurer uses [[Smoothing|smoothing]] to change bonus rates gradually overtime rather than suddenly, which helps meet [[Policyholders’ Reasonable Expectations (PRE)]].
>- Calculation methods:
>	- *Simple*: the bonus is a percentage of the basic benefit only
>	- *Compound*: the bonus is a percentage of the basic benefit plus any previously attached bonuses.
>	- *Super-compound*: The bonus uses two percentages - one applied to the basic benefit and a second (typically higher) percentage applied to previous bonuses. This method **defers the distribution of surplus** as compared to *simple* or *compound*.
>	
>![[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Diagrams/Pasted image 20260127071545.png]]
>- ***Capital Efficiency*:** Methods that defer the build-up of these guarantees, such as the super-compound method, are more **capital efficient** because they keep supervisory reserves lower in the early years.
  
> [!special] Special reversionary bonus
> A **once-off** bonus declared on an ad-hoc basis in addition to any regular reversionary bonus.
>- ***Managing expectations of policyholders***: The primary intention of declaring a bonus as "special" is to grant an increase in benefits without creating a reasonable expectation that similar increases will be granted in the future. 
>- ***Use cases***: Used to distribute surplus from sources that are unlikely to arise again, for example:
>	- The restructuring of a with-profits fund
>	- Surplus from a demutualisation or a takeover

> [!Fail] Terminal bonuses
> A terminal bonus is an additional profit distribution paid only when the contract reaches maturity, or upon death or surrender.
> - ***Purpose***: Used to distribute other sources of surplus such as
> 	- volatile sources e.g. capital gains 
> 	- withdrawal 
> 	- expense and 
> 	- mortality profits
>- ***Deferral***: The primary purpose of a terminal bonus is that it defers the distribution of surplus until the very end of the contract. This slows the build-up of fixed guarantees, providing the insurer with greater investment freedom to invest in volatile higher returning assets like equities.
>- ***Link to asset share***: Ensures that the final payout is equal to the policy's asset share. (the accumulated net cashflow of the policy)
>- ***Calculation method***: It is often expressed as a percentage of of total reversionary bonuses or total claim amount before the terminal bonus is added.
>- ***Lack of guarantee***: Unlike reversionary bonuses, terminal bonuses are not guaranteed until the insured event actually occurs. Insurers typically set the terminal bonus rates annually but may decrease them suddenly in response to market crashes.

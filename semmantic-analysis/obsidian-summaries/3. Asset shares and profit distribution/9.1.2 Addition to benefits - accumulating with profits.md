
> [!steps] Regular annual bonuses
> In [[Accumulating With-profits]] contracts, bonuses are added annually based on the total of premiums paid to date plus any bonuses that have been declared. These bonuses act like interest:
> $$ F_{t+1} = (F_t + P_t - c_t)(1 + b_t)$$
> Where:
> - F is the Benefit/Fund value
> - P is the Premium
> - c represents charges
> - b is the regular bonus rate
>
>There is an explicit relationship between the premiums paid and the addition to the benefit (allocation rate). These contracts are often unitised, however it is not necessary for the contract to be unitised

> [!info] Unitised with-profits
> If the contract is structured as [[Unitised with-profit|unitised with-profits]], the regular bonuses are delivered in one of two ways:
> - **Constant unit price**: the price of a unit remains fixed and the insurer allocates **additional units** to each contract (usually annually) at the bonus declarations. There could be a minimum bonus declaration of zero or some positive percentage. 
> - **Variable unit pric**e: The insurer changes the price of the units (usually on a daily basis). The increase is made up of a guaranteed part (possibly zero) and a bonus part.
>

>[!trending-up] Smoothing and discretion
>Unlike pure unit-linked business, the increases are **not directly linked to a specific pool of assets**, instead the insurer uses actuarial discretion to take a long-term view of investment returns and smooths the bonuses to protect policyholders from market volatility.

> [!missing] Terminal bonuses
> This is added only when the policy becomes a claim (maturity, death or surrender) and is intended to bring the final payout closer to the policy's actual asset share. 

> [!info] Market Value Reduction (MRV)
> The MVR is a discretionary reduction applied by the company at the time of surrender to ensure the payout does not exceed the policy's asset share. 
> 
> By applying an MVR, the company protects the interests of the remaining policyholders by ensuring that withdrawing individuals do not leave with more than the assets backing their contracts.  

> [!receipt] Use of the asset share
The [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/3. Asset shares and profit distribution/8.1 Asset shares|asset share]] is particularly important for helping to determine terminal bonus rates and in assessing the equity of any proposed bonus distribution.

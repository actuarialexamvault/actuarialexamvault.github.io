### Comparison of Surplus Distribution Systems

| Feature                      | Additions to Benefits (UK Style)                                                                                    | Revalorisation                                                                                                         | Contribution Method                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Equity & Smoothing**       | **High Smoothing.** 🌊 Equity is managed over the long term, often using Terminal Bonuses to ensure final fairness. | **Low Smoothing.** ⚖️ Equity is high in a "current year" sense because profits are distributed as earned via interest. | **Highest Equity.** 🎯 Aims to return surplus to the specific group that generated it (Mortality, Expense, and Interest).  |
| **Flexibility (Discretion)** | **High.** 🎛️ The actuary has significant discretion over bonus rates and smoothing levels.                         | **Low.** 🧱 Usually governed by a strict formula.                                                                      | **Moderate.** ⚙️ Formulaic, but the insurer can adjust the "factors" used in the three-factor dividend calculation.        |
| **Simplicity**               | **Simple for Users.** 😊 Easy to understand "bonuses," but the underlying math can be complex (Low transparency).   | **Very Simple.** 📖 Direct link to investment performance; very transparent for the policyholder.                      | **Complex.** 🧩 Requires detailed Analysis of Surplus (AoS) to calculate components for mortality, expenses, and interest. |
| **Investment Freedom**       | **High.** 🚀 Because of smoothing, the office can invest in volatile assets (Equities/Property).                    | **Low.** 📉 Must often match the "Technical Rate" with fixed-interest assets to ensure the formula can be met.         | **Moderate.** 🏦 Depends on the guarantee levels, but generally more restrictive than smoothed funds.                      |

> [!info] The "Smoothing" Trade-off 
> In **Additions to Benefits**, smoothing allows you to pay out more than the [[Asset share]] in bad years, funded by the "estate." In **Revalorisation**, the lack of smoothing means if the market crashes, the credit to the policyholders crashes immediately too.

> [!info] Margins for Future Adverse Experience 
> The insurer must ensure that the surplus distributed today does not leave the fund vulnerable tomorrow.
> 
> - With-profits premium rates are typically designed with implicit or explicit margins to generate profit under normal conditions. These margins effectively serve as a **buffer against adverse future experience**.
> -If experience (such as mortality or investment return) is worse than anticipated, the insurer can theoretically reduce bonuses to compensate for the loss. 
> 
> However, there are several limitations to this:
>	- Choice of distribution method e.g. [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/3. Asset shares and profit distribution/9.1.3 Revalorisation|Revalorisation]]
>	- [[Policyholders’ Reasonable Expectations (PRE)]] 
>	- [[Guarantee|Guarantees]]
>     

> [!info] Business Objectives 
> A primary business objective is to **maximize profit distribution to policyholders** to improve the company's competitive position. Competitive pressure encourages high bonus rates to attract new business. However, this creates a conflict during financial instability:
>
>• Reductions in bonus rates can injure a company's competitive standing and reduce new business volumes.
>
>• Management must balance this against the need to **ensure solvency** or reflect "financial reality," which may necessitate reducing rates.
>
>• The level of control over this risk varies by method; for example, the **conventional additions to benefits method** allows the actuary to smooth rates to manage short-term anomalous results, whereas the revalorisation method offers no such discretion
> - **Growth vs. Yield:** If the objective is rapid growth, the company might pay higher bonuses to improve **marketability** and attract new business. 📈
>     
> - **Proprietary vs. Mutual:** In a proprietary company, a portion of the distributed surplus (often 1/9th of the bonus) is transferred to **shareholders**, creating a conflict between policyholder returns and shareholder dividends. 🏛️
>     

> [!info] Policyholders' Reasonable Expectations (PRE) 
>Policyholders develop **Reasonable Expectations** regarding the level and form of their bonuses based on three sources: company documentation (marketing and projections), the company’s past practice (such as smoothing history), and general market practice (bonuses declared by competitors).
>
>• **Management Response:** If a company finds a larger-than-expected surplus, it may avoid declaring it all at once to prevent setting a repeatable high expectation that cannot be sustained.
>
>• **Smoothing Risks:** Conversely, a lower-than-expected surplus often leads to "over-payment" of profit to maintain smoothing expectations. However, if this persists, it can compromise the company’s financial security.
>
>• **Fairness:** Failure to meet market-consistent expectations can lead to policyholder dissatisfaction and regulatory intervention for failing to **treat customers fairly**

> [!info] Provision of Capital 
> The timing of profit distribution affects the insurer's **free assets**. If profit distribution is deferred (delayed), the retained funds augment the company's free assets in the meantime.
>
>• **Investment Freedom:** Greater deferral—achieved through **terminal bonuses** or **super-compound reversionary bonuses**—gives the company greater investment freedom to seek higher returns in riskier assets like equities or property.
>
>• **Financing New Business:** These "extra" free assets can be used to support the **new business strain** of selling new policies.
>
>• **Distribution Mismatch:** Over time, there must be a balance; sustained over-distribution of bonuses (distributing more than is generated) will lead to an excessive drain on free assets and the company's capital.
>
• **Method Comparisons:** The conventional additions to benefits method allows for significant deferral. In contrast, accumulating with-profits and contribution methods generally defer less profit because regular distributions are expressed in **current money terms** (interest rates or cash dividends), which are harder to withhold without hurting marketability

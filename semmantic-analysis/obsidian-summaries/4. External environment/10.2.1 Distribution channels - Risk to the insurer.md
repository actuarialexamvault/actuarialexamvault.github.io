
> [!caution] Impact on risk to the insurer
> The **choice of distribution** channel introduces several macro risks, primarily related to demographic profile, persistency and anti-selection.
> - ***[[Anti-selection]]***: intermediaries are subject to the most stringent underwriting because they represent the client, not the insurer, increasing the potential for anti-selection. Conversely, direct marketing involves the lowest level of underwriting to avoid barriers to sales, but the insurer risks attracting a disproportionate share of high-risk applicants because it appears to have the most relaxed standards in the markets.
> - ***[[Withdrawal (Persistency) risk]]***: Channels using aggressive sales approaches or reaching less sophisticated buyers are more likely to see early lapses. Intermediaries typically have the lowest withdrawal rates.
> - **Mix of business risk***: Insurers set pricing assumptions based on an expected mix of business. If the volume of a specific channel changes unexpectedly, the overall experience (mortality, morbidity, and expenses) may deviate from the pricing basis. 
> - **[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Expense risk|Expense risk]]**: Direct marketing and own sales forces involve high fixed overheads and development costs. If these channels produce low sales volumes, the company may fail to recover the costs, while excessive volume can overstretch capital resources and administrative capacity.
> - ***Intermediary actions (lapse and re-entry)***: independent distributors may act in their own interest by encouraging clients to cancel their policies and take out new ones, which can cause significant financial loss to the original insurer. This can be mitigated by exit penalties or commission clawbacks. 

> [!group] Impact on demographic profile
> The primary effect on the demographic profile is known as **[[class selection]]**, where different channels attract groups with distinct characteristics.
> - ***Income and sophistication***: Independent intermediaries typically reach wealthier and more financially sophisticated clients. In contrast direct marking often reaches less sophisticated customers with lower income levels.
> - ***Risk correlation***: The demographic profile correlates directly with mortality, sickness and withdrawal experience. For example, wealthier clients reached by intermediaries may show lighter mortality due to better lifestyles and healthcare access. Conversely, aggressive selling to less sophisticated buyers can lead to higher withdrawal rates.

> [!example] Impact on contract design
> Contract design must be aligned with the distribution channel's ability to communicate the product's value to the target market.
> - ***Complexity vs simplicity***: Channels catering to sophisticated markets can support complex product designs. However, products sold via direct marketing must be simple and easy to understand.
> - ***Risk correlation***: Lack of clarity about the features of the product can hinder sales, lead to mis-selling risks or failing to meet [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Policyholders’ Reasonable Expectations (PRE)|Policyholders’ Reasonable Expectations (PRE)]].

> [!receipt] Impact on pricing
> The distribution channel influences the pricing basis through its effect on expected experience and competitive requirements.
> - ***Underwriting and claims***: The stringency of underwriting usually varies by channel, intermediaries face the most stringent checks, while direct marketing involves the lowest level of underwriting. These differences must be reflected in the mortality and morbidity assumptions used in pricing.
> - ***Competitiveness***: The need for competitive rates is greatest when selling through independent intermediaries, as they directly compare the company's rates against the rest of the market. 
> - ***Expense loading***: Remuneration varies across channels, for instance high initial commissions for intermediaries or agents create a large new business strain that must be recouped through higher expense loading in the premium.
> - ***Weighted assumptions***: If an insurer uses multiple channels for the same product, it must use a **weighted average of the experience based on the expected mix of business** by source. If the actual mix deviates from these assumptions, the company faces a risk to its projected profitability.
> 

> [!INFO] Note on competitiveness
> Having competitive rates or charges is not the be-all and end-all for every product, even
when selling through intermediaries. For example:
>- Products may be differentiated from the competition through innovative features
or attractive options.
>- More complex products may be difficult to compare across companies.
>- Some savings contracts may compete as much on past investment performance
as on premium rates or charges.
>- Some products may compete on the level of customer service or admin support –
eg for group life or pension products.

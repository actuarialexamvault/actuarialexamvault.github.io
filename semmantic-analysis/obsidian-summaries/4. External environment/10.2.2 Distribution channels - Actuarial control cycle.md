
> [!cycle] Using the control cycle to manage distribution channels
> The [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Actuarial Control Cycle|Actuarial Control Cycle]] can be used at a **macro level** to **specify the problem** by helping a company decide which distribution channels such as **independent intermediaries, tied agents, an own sales force, or direct marketing**, should be used to achieve future profitability while maintaining an acceptable level of risk. 
> 
> This involves analyzing the **general commercial environment and competitors** to identify successful methods that align with the company's strategic goals. 
> 
> In the **developing the solution** stage, the choice of distribution channel serves as a critical input for projection models, as it dictates **assumptions** regarding the **volume, type, and mix of new business**, average policy size, and demographic factors like **mortality, morbidity, and withdrawal rates**. These models must be used to test the impact of variations from central assumptions, particularly for highly uncertain elements like future new business levels, before a specific strategy is adopted. 
> 
> The **monitoring and feedback** phase requires the company to continually analyze actual experience to see if the type and quality of business coming onto the books materializes as expected in the models. If actual experience deviates from the assumed parameters, the actuary must update the model assumptions, which may lead to revised management decisions. 
> 
> At a **micro level**, the control cycle helps manage the distribution by evaluating the performance of individual channels, such as specific brokers or agents, to identify and improve areas of poor performance through **better training, administration, or sales information**. 
> 
> Furthermore, the element of **professionalism** within the cycle ensures that the sales strategy is ethical and aligned with **consumer interests**, requiring the insurer to identify and rectify poor practices while fostering good ones. Overall, the control cycle ensures the distribution strategy is **sound and sustainable**.


> [!receipt] Types of expenses
> Companies categorize their operating costs into two streams: 
> - Commissions payable to salespeople and 
> - Management expenses.

> [!info] Management expenses 
> Management expenses are generally split based on the stage of the policy:
> - Initial (Acquisition) Expenses
> - Renewal (Maintenance) Expenses
> - Termination (Claim) Expenses

> [!summary] Commissions
> Commissions are the primary way sales channels are renumerated.
> - Initial commission (major driver of [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/New Business Strain|New Business Strain]])
> - Renewal commission (may be clawed back on early lapse)

> [!EXAMPLE] Fixed vs Marginal (Variable) Costs
> - ***Marginal (variable) costs***: are costs incurred only because a policy exists e.g. commission
>- ***Fixed costs (overheads)***: incurred regardless of the volume of business e.g. management salaries, IT, HR and Premises
>
>This distinction creates: 
>- **Volume risk**: since overheads are spread across the policy portfolio, the **actual average expense per policy** will rise if fewer policies are sold than assumed in the pricing basis. Conversely, unexpectedly high volumes can **overstretch administrative resources**, leading to inefficiencies

> [!trending-up] The influence of inflation
> Inflation is the primary driver of [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Expense risk|Expense risk]], particularly for long-term guarantees like whole life where premiums are often level and fixed at the outset.
>- ***Earnings inflation***: because a vast majority of a life office's operating costs are related to staff salaries, expenses are more sensitive to earnings inflation than general price inflation.
>- ***Erosion of loadings***: if inflation is higher than the fixed expense loadings built into the premiums, the cost of administering the policy will eventually exceed the amount of premium collected.
>- ***Consistency in modelling***: when setting assumptions, the inflation rate must be consistent with the future investment income assumption. A differential between the return on government fixed-interest secirities and index-linked securities is often used to estimate future inflation expectations.
> 

 > [!solvency] Impact on capital and profitability
 > The combination of high initial expenses and the requirement to set up prudent **supervisory reserves** often results in **negative cash flow in the first year**, known as **new business strain**. 
 > 
 > To manage this, insurers must ensure that the charges received over the life of the policy are sufficient to recoup these up-front outlays. If charges are linked to fund values (as in unit-linked business), poor investment performance can reduce the monetary value of those charges, creating a shortfall to meet fixed expenses.

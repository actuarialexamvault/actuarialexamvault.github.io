
> [!abstract] Legal constraints
> **The legal environment** acts as a significant external constraint by limiting an insurer’s **discretion** and ensuring the **protection of policyholders**.

> [!trajectories] Constraints on insurers discretion
> In cases where the insurer has discretionary power, such as declaring bonuses or reviewing charges, the legal environment constraints this flexibility through by requiring the insurer to be consistent with [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Policyholders’ Reasonable Expectations (PRE)|Policyholders’ Reasonable Expectations (PRE)]], which can have legal force through court rulings.

> [!example] Contract law constraints
> Contract law may **void** specific clauses if they are deemed **unfair**. Such as:
> - Voiding reviewability clauses
> - Mis-representations e.g. by intermediaries or marketing brochures.
> - New-legislation that a retrospective force

> [!solvency] Regulatory requirements
> Regulators may impose restrictions on life insurance companies, usually with the aim of protecting policyholders. 
> Common restrictions on:
> 
> **Product design**
> - ***Contracts types***: A restriction on the types of contracts that a life insurance company can offer
> - ***Premium rates***: Restrictions on the premium rates or charges for certain types of contracts
> - ***Terms and conditions***: Requirements relating to the terms and conditions of the contracts
> - ***Method of sale***: Restrictions on the channels through which life insurance can be sold, on sales procedures or on information given at the point of sale.
> - ***Non-Discrimination***: Restrictions on **rating factors** and the ability to underwrite (e.g. to avoid discrimination)  
>  
>  
>**Capital and reserving**
>  - **Solvency capital requirements**: minimum reserving or solvency margin requirements 
>  - ***Asset Valuation methods***: dictate whether certain assets are valued at **market value** (active approach) or **historic/book value** (passive approach), which directly impacts the reported solvency position
> 
> **Investment strategy**
> - limit mismatching liabilities
> - restrict asset types  
> - restrict concentration on a single asset type
> - Maximum counterparty exposure
> - Mismatching reserves
> - tax treatment of different asset classes
> - require independent custodians
> - admissible assets
> - liquidity requirement (cash holding)
> - restrict foreign investment
> - prescribe asset classes
> - method of calculating asset values (fair value)
>
> **Disclosures**
> - Point of sale and marketing materials
> 	- Projection/illustrations e.g. maturity/surrender values
> 	- Charging structures
> 	- Reviewability (yes/no)
> 	- Risk disclosure (risks to the policyholder)
> - Financial and solvency 
> 	- Published accounts (true and fair view)
> 	- Supervisory returns (SCR)
> 	- EV in published accounts
> 	- Impact of basis changes
> - Market-consistent valuation
> 	- disclose how stochastic models are calibrated: risk-neutral vs real-world calibration
>

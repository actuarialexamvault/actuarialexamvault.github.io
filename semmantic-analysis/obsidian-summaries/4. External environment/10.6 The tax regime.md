
> [!summary] Taxation
> The most common methods through which governments tax life insurance companies are:
> - ***Company tax***: a tax on the annual profits of the business (taxing the **increase in free (own) assets**)
> - ***Investment income tax (I-E)***: a tax payable on the investment income (including capital gains) less some or all operating expenses.
> - ***Premium Tax:*** Some regimes also impose a direct tax on premium income

> [!group] Impact on consumer propensity and marketability
> The tax regime is a major driver of consumer buying habits. The attractiveness of an insurance products depends on:
> 1. Whether premiums are tax-deductible
> 2. Whether the insurer's internal funds are taxed during the contract
> 3. Whether the eventual benefits are taxable in the hands of the policyholder.
> 
> Tax incentives such as deductible premiums or tax free benefits, can subsidize life insurance savings, making them more favorable than bank accounts or direct equity.

> [!caution] Product design constraints and opportunities
> Tax authorities often impose **minimum standards** to ensure that pure savings products are not dressed up as insurance simply to **evade tax**, such as **minimum level of life cover**.
> 
> Actuaries must ensure **consistency** in their pricing models, if a company is taxed on "I-E" basis, the investment income and expense assumptions must both reflect the **net-of-tax treatment**. 

> [!danger] Taxation as a source of risk
> The long-term nature of life insurance contracts exposes insurers to **taxation risk**, as fiscal rules can change over the decades a policy remains in force.
>
>- **Tax Changes:** Existing policies are generally **not immune** to changes in tax methods or rates, meaning an insurer may realize less net profit than anticipated if the fiscal environment worsens after the policy is issued.
>
>- **Parameter Sensitivity:** For contracts with fixed, guaranteed benefits, the insurer bears the risk that higher corporate or investment taxes will erode the margins intended for expenses and profit.
>
>- **Asset Share Deductions:** In with-profits business, the **asset share** must explicitly deduct tax on investment income and allow for accrued tax liabilities on unrealized capital gains to ensure fairness between policyholders


> [!info] Actuarial associations and guidance as an external constraint
> Actuarial associations issue professional guidance to provide a **framework of considerations** for actuaries carrying out their responsibilities. As an external constraint, professional guidance limits the discretion of company management and actuaries in several ways:
> - **Protecting Actuaries Against Commercial Pressure:** Guidance provides a safeguard for actuaries against pressure from company proprietors or shareholders to agree to actions that may **prejudice policyholder interests**
> - **Capital and Solvency Oversight:** A key example of this constraint involves planned new business levels; if a company's growth plans require more capital than is available, professional guidance empowers the actuary to argue for a change in strategy to **ensure long-term survival**.
> - **Ethical Distribution Standards:** Professional guidance empowers actuaries to require insurers to identify and rectify poor sales practices that are not **ethical and aligned with consumer interests**.

> [!check] Professional guidance as an opportunity
> Professional guidance is not merely a restriction but also creates significant opportunities for the industry:
> - **Generating Consumer Confidence:** By adding safeguards for the proper running of life insurers, professional guidance generates the **public confidence** necessary for a thriving industry.
>
>- **Product Flexibility and Cost:** When **professional standards are high**, the regulatory regime can remain appropriate rather than becoming over-costly or undue. This environment allows insurers to offer **lower-cost and more flexible products** to consumers.
>
>- **Market Growth:** Ultimately, professional guidance and regulation share the same goal: creating a stable environment that encourages consumers to **invest in life insurance products**


> [!summary]
>Policy and other data are fundamental sources of risk because they form the basis for all actuarial investigations and strategic decision-making. If data is flawed, reality will turn out differently than expected, potentially leading to **inappropriate decisions regarding pricing, product design, investment, and reinsurance**
> 

> [!example] Policy data risks
> A life insurer maintains a database of in-force policies which contains critical information like age at entry, policy term and sum assured.
>- ***Data quality***: There is a risk that the company fails to maintain **adequate, accurate and complete** records which can lead to inaccurate valuations of liabilities. 
>- ***Model point representation***: For some investigations, model points representing all or part of the business may be used. There will be a risk that these model points do not adequately represent the true distribution of the business. e.g. misrepresenting average age or sum assured
>- ***Model risk***: Even with accurate representation, there is a risk that the model is an over-simplification of the real company, causing it to be unrealistic.

> [!info] Other data used in assumption setting
> Data used to determine actuarial assumptions (such as mortality, morbidity or expenses) often comes from **internal** experience or **external** sources.
> - **Credibility**: there may not be enough data for it to be statistically credible.
> - ***Quality***: there may be **missing data** or the data contains **inaccurate** information
> - ***Relevance***: Data may inapplicable to the specific population being modeled, due to **differences between the population** on which it was based and the population for which it will be used. For example, [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/Why using expense data from with-profits endowments to price term assurance is risky]]
> - ***Future assumptions***: Even if the data is of good quality, it may not be suitable to base future assumptions of it (unless its significantly modified).
> - ***External data***: industry or national statistics may be **misleading** for a company if the external population has a **different target market or underwriting standards** than the insurer's own policyholder population.

> [!caution] Data issues for health and care contracts
> Data risks are **more severe** for health and care products (IP, CI and LTCI) due to:
> - ***Limited volumes***: CI and LTCI typically have lower *policy volumes* and lower *incidence rates*, which limits the statistical credibility of the available data
> - ***Heterogeneity*** of products and markets limits the applicability of industry data
> - ***Rapid changes*** to products and markets over time limits the applicability of historical data ^data-issues-for-health-and-care-contracts

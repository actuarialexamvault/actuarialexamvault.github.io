
> [!caution] Risks from distributors
> Distributors, such as insurance intermediaries and brokers, can introduce financial and operational risks through several behaviors:
>
>• **Lapse and Re-entry:** Distributors may encourage policyholders to cancel existing policies and take out new ones if there are no exit penalties or commission "clawbacks". This results in a loss for the insurer if the profit from the new policy fails to cover the loss of future profits from the original contract and the new acquisition costs.
>
>• **Exploitation of Product Design:** Intermediaries may take advantage of **loopholes in risk classification**, such as finding ways to get a policyholder accepted at non-smoker rates despite their actual health status.
>
>• **Unit Pricing Arbitrage:** In unit-linked business, distributors might exploit **timing differences in unit pricing** by cashing in units after a market fall but before the insurer has updated the daily price.
>
>• **Credit Risk (Broker Balances):** Insurers are exposed to the risk that brokers collect premiums from policyholders but **delay or fail to pass those funds** to the company.
>
>• **Mis-selling and Legal Risk:** If a distributor makes representations to a client that are **inconsistent with the policy document**, the insurer faces potential legal action, regulatory intervention, and reputational damage.

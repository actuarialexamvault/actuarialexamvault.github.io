
> [!fail] Failure of management systems and controls
> Risk arises from the possibility that directors fail to impose or adequately monitor **systems of control**. Even if systems are documented, they may be contravened due to insufficient monitoring, leading to **financial losses, regulatory intervention, and reputational damage**. For instance, a failure to implement a pricing control system correctly might result in the company selling loss-making products

> [!example] Fraud
>- ***Directors or staff***: Because directors have **special access to financial and banking systems**, as well as the computer programs and data supporting the business, they are in a position to perpetrate deliberate fraud. This type of control failure caused by **deliberate intent** can lead to immediate and severe losses for the insurer.
>- ***Policyholders***: the main risk here relates to fraudulent claims, the risk increasing in proportion to the difficulty of detection. Countries with secure processes for certifying deaths represent reduced risks.
>- ***Other outside parties***: such parties may effectively obtain some access to the computer systems of the insurer, particularly where there are external components such as website access.

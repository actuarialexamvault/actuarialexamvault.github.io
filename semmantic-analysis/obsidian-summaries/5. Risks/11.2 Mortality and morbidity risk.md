
> [!skull] Components of mortality/morbidity risk
> There are three risks associated with [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Mortality Risk|mortality]]/[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/morbidity]] assumptions:
> - ***Model risk***: the assumed probability distribution could be inappropriate e.g assuming policyholders are i.i.d, with  $Bin(n, p)$. The failure of these assumptions about the population i.e. *independent, identical and the type of distribution* with respect to mortality would increase the risk.
> - ***Parameter risk***: the specific model parameter estimates e.g. $p$  used in the model may not reflect actual population parameters even though the underlying model is appropriate.
> - ***Random fluctuations***: unpredictable variations due to sample size (too low for the law of large numbers to apply).
>
>The likelihood of our assumptions for the future being correct depends on the **quality, credibility and relevance** of the data available.

> [!cycle] Influences on model and parameter uncertainty
> Insurers must constantly update their assumptions, but several factors make this process risky:
> - Medical advances
> - Data inadequacy
> - [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Expanding funnel of doubt|Expanding funnel of doubt]]
> - Capital and reserving impact

> [!trajectories] Directions of mortality risk
> The impact of mortality depends on the nature of the insurance contract:
> - **Mortality**: For life insurance products like term assurance, the risk is that deaths are higher than expected, leading to a reduction in profit or capital shortfall. The risk is most significant at the start of a contract when the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Sum at risk|sum at risk]] is at its highest.
> - **Longevity**: For survival-contingent products like immediate annuities, the risk is that policyholders live longer than expected. In this scenario, the insurer risks paying out more income than they had initially priced for through the initial single premium and reserved for.
>
>See mortality question example: [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/Mortality risk risk on a without-profit term assurance, for an insurance company in an established market within a developed economy|Question 13.3]]

> [!lapse] Selection risks
> Mortality experience is heavily influenced by the **behaviour** and **health status** of policyholders:
>- [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Anti-selection|Anti-selection]]
>- [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Selective withdrawal|Selective withdrawals]]  

> [!old] Claims experience for health and care products
> Claims experience for IP, CI and LTCI is significantly more difficult to manage and model because benefits are contingent on health states rather than death.
> - The primary risk for health and care contracts is due to the **scarcity of reliable, credible and relevant data** due to [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/5. Risks/11.1 Data risks#^data-issues-for-health-and-care-contracts| data issues for health and care contracts]].
> - The primary risk for IP involves **transfer probabilities** in a multi-state model: the rate at which healthy lives fall sick (inception) and the rate at which sick lives recover or die (termination)
> - For CI, the main risk is the **rate of diagnosis** for the specific illnesses listed in the contract.
> - The main risk for LTCI relates to claim **inception rates** and the probabilities of **moving between different states of care**,. e.g., from home care to nursing home care.

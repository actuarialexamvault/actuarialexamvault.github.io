
> [!receipt] Components of expense inflation risk
> When assumptions are made about **future expenses** to be incurred by the company, these will allow for inflation (either on a deterministic or stochastic basis) . There is therefore a **parameter risk**. In addition there will be a **model risk** if stochastic assumptions are being used. Inflation assumptions are also subject to an [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Expanding funnel of doubt|Expanding funnel of doubt]] and **random fluctuations**. 

> [!tied] The link between inflation and investment returns
> The differential between the return on government fixed-interest securities and index-linked securities is a specific [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/The link between inflation and investment returns|link]] used to estimate inflation expectations. Model risk is high if inflation and investment returns are not **dynamically linked** within the model.

> [!caution] Sources of expense risk
> Expense risk stems from the possibility that future reality will differ from the assumptions made when the products were originally priced. The risk that the charges/loadings received **are lower than expected** is typically classified as other types of risk depending on the driver of the shortfall:
>1. ***New business mix or volume risk***: fixed overheads must be spread over fewer policies, the actual per-policy cost will rise above the level assumed in pricing. Conversely. inappropriately high volumes can overstretch administrative resources leading to inefficiencies and mistakes
>2. ***Inflation risk***: if inflation is higher than expected, the fixed expense loadings built into premiums (especially on level premiums) may become insufficient to cover actual escalating costs.
>3. [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Withdrawal (Persistency) risk|Withdrawal (Persistency) risk]]: if a policy withdraws early, the insurer may fail to receive enough premiums to recover initial expenses
>4. [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/5. Risks/11.3 Investment risk|Investment risk]]: in unit linked contracts where charges are a percentage of the fund value, a drop in investment returns reduces the value of those charges, creating a shortfall for meeting fixed expenses.

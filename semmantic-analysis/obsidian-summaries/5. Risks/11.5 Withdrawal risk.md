
>[!lapse] Components of withdrawal risk
> The estimation of future withdrawals which encompasses surrenders, lapses and making policies paid up is a fundamental source of risk for insurers, primarily because policyholder behaviour often deviates from actuarial assumptions. The risks in the estimations include:
> - ***Model risk***: arises when an inappropriate framework to predict behaviour is used. such as incorrectly assuming that withdrawal rates are linked to specific variables like investment returns or GDP. 
> - ***Parameter risk***: even if the model is correct, there is a risk that the parameters used (the specific rates) are wrongly estimated, such as generally underestimating the level of future withdrawals. 
> - ***Random fluctuations***: in smaller insurance portfolios, the actual number of withdrawals can vary unpredictable due to sample error, even if the underlying parameters and model are correct. 

> [!skull] Withdrawals as a driver of mortality risk
> In actuarial investigations, risks are **dynamically linked**, withdrawals are treated as a **driver variable** for mortality. In a multiple decrement model, **mortality and withdrawal are dependent rates** due to the effect of [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Selective withdrawal|selective withdrawals]]. If the withdrawal parameter is wrongly estimated, the mortality rate becomes fundamentally incorrect.

> [!receipt] Withdrawals as a driver of expense risk
> A similar relationship exists between withdrawal rates and expense parameters.
> 1. Spreading of fixed costs
> - Expense assumptions for **in-force business** are calibrated based on a model of future **new business and withdrawals**
> - If actual withdrawals are higher than the modelled parameter (the withdrawal rate), the company's **fixed overhead costs** must be spread over fewer policies. 
> - This invalidates the previous expense parameter, as the **actual per-policy expense** becomes higher than what was allowed for in the pricing or reserving basis.
> 
> The relationship between withdrawals and expenses is a **direct dynamic mathematical link** driven by the **allocation of fixed costs**.
>  
> 2. Recouping initial expenses
>- The pricing model calculates charges/premiums based on a specific assumed withdrawal rate to ensure the company receives enough income over time to cover the **initial capital strain**. 
>- When actual withdrawal behaviour departs from the estimate and policyholders leave earlier than expected,
>- The future charges required to repay the initial acquisition costs are never received - the company incurs an **immediate loss**.
>
>While the is a financial shortfall is in the company's ability to cover its expenses, it is specifically classified as [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Withdrawal (Persistency) risk|Withdrawal (Persistency) risk]].
>  
>

> [!trending-down] Withdrawals as a driver of investment risk
> Withdrawal estimation directly influences the discounted mean term of liabilities which can lead to [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/5. Risks/11.3 Investment risk#^233c89|mismatching risk]].

> [!solvency] What is the impact of changes in lapse assumptions on the reserves?
> Lapses have opposing effects on different elements of the reserve.
> - Future premiums
> - Future benefits
> - Future expenses
> - Negative reserves
> - The overall effect is uncertain

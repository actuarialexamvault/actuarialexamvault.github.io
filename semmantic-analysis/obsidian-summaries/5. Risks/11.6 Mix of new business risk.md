
New business mix is a significant risk because new policies taken on by a company gradually changes its **risk and profitability characteristics** over time. An unintended change in this mix can lead to a **shift in the risk profile and capital needs** that may exceed the resources available to the company.

> [!summary] Mix by Nature 
> The "nature" of a contract refers to its fundamental structure and classification. Changes in this mix include shifts between:
> - Class of between: e.g. the proportion of pensions business written
> - Type of contract: e.g. term assurances vs endowments
> - Contract design: e.g. with-profits vs unit linked
> - Premium frequency: single vs regular premium
>
>When the capital required to write these different products or the inherent risks (such as investment or mortality risk) vary across these categories, the company's overall capital needs and risk exposure will change along with the fluctuations.
>

> [!trending-down] Mix by size 
> The risk related to contract size primary concerns expense recovery. Many insurance expenses are **fixed per policy**, meaning they are incurred at the same level regardless of the policy's sum assured or premium size. Conversely, the income from **charges and premium loadings** is often a proportion of the premium.
> - ***Average size reduction***: If new contracts are **smaller on average** than assumed in pricing, the total income from charges will be reduced, but expenses will not decrease by the same margin, leading to a mismatch the company may fail to cover its fixed costs.
> - ***Cross-subsidies***: Companies often accept a degree of cross-subsidy where larger contracts subsidize the fixed costs of smaller ones. An unintended shift toward smaller policies erodes this subsidy potentially making the product line loss making. 

> [!call] Mix by Source (distribution channel)
>  The [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/4. External environment/10.2 Distribution channels#^b15fc8|distribution channel]] used to sell the policy, is a source of risk because:
>  - ***[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/4. External environment/10.2.1 Distribution channels - Risk to the insurer#^3dca96|Demographics]]:*** different channels reach different populations, with varying mortality, morbidity and withdrawal experience.
>  - ***Distribution costs***: salespeople's remuneration and commission levels vary by channel 
>  
>  An insurer may wish to charge the same price for a product through all its distribution channels despite the different expected experience (due to the above). It would therefore set mortality and expense assumptions based on a **weighted average** of the expected mix of business by source. If the actual mix differs from the expected source, these parameters may be **invalidated**, leading to less profit than anticipated or even suffer a loss. To mitigate the risk arising for the mix by source, the company can price its products differently for each distribution channel ([[dual pricing]]), as each channel remains independently profitable.
>

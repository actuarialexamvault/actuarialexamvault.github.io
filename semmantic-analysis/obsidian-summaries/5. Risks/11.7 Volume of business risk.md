
> [!trending-up] Too high volumes
> - [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/New Business Strain#^2bbbc5|Initial capital strain]] will use up the company's free assets (the excess of assets over liabilities), potentially leading to a situation where capital resources become inadequate to support further growth or continued solvency.
> - Rapid growth can over-stretch the company's **administrative resources**, leading to inefficiencies, processing delays or underwriting mistakes. These failure can damage the company's reputation and create secondary marketing risks.

> [!trending-down] Too low volumes
> - ***Failure to cover fixed overheads***: if volumes are lower than expected, the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/Why expense assumptions for in-force business may depend on a model of future new business volumes|actual average expense per policy]] will rise, eroding profit margins.
> - ***Recovering development costs***: If sales are too low, the company risks never recovering the up-front costs of developing and marketing the product line.
> - ***Long-term viability***: a significant fall in business volumes will damage the company's competitiveness and eventually threaten its long term viability as it becomes less able to achieve **economies of scale**.

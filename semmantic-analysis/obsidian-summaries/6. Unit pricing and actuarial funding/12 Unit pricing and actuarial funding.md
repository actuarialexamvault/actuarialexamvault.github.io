
> [!abstract] Unit pricing
> This whole chapter is concerned with one aspect of a [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/1. Life insurance contracts/4.1 Unit-linked contracts|unit-linked]] product.
>- A unit-linked benefit is determined by the performance of units. This performance is determined by the performance of the **underlying assets**.
>- Pricing a unit is assessing the value of assets attributable to one unit in the fund. 

> [!example] Example: A fund has R200 worth of assets and 10 units, the price would be R20 per unit. 

>[!explainer] The basic idea is that the ***value*** of the pool of assets is divided by the ***number*** of units

>[!caution] Principal risks in unit pricing
>- ***Timing risk***: Unit prices are set at discrete points in time, while asset values move continuously
>	- Asset values are volatile
>	- Sudden movements in asset values
>	- Outdated asset valuations (property)
>- ***Approximations***
>	- tax on unrealised gains
>	- accrued interest
>- ***Errors***: Incorrect data/calculation errors
>- ***Wrong basis*** (appropriation vs expropriation price)
>- ***Regulatory changes*** (restrictions on charges included in the unit price)
>- ***Reputational risk***: from violating basic equity principle or unclear communication
>- ***Anti-selection:*** Policyholder selection against the company if the price overstates the asset value

> [!important] Bid and offer
> The words **bid** and **offer** are used in three different situations:
> - to refer to the buying and selling prices of assets
> - to refer to the buying and selling prices of units
> - to refer to the basis used to price units

> [!abstract] Actuarial funding
> Is the idea that, for unit-linked policies that have level (ongoing) charges to cover initial expenses, we can reduce the first year's capital strain by holding an adjusted reserve, by taking into account the expected income from charges to reduce the initial capital strain:
>
> - [ Adjusted reserve ] = [Normal reserve] - EPV[Initial expense charges]


> [!receipt] Appropriation and expropriation prices
> To maintain this equity, insurers distinguish between the price at which units are created and the price at which they are cancelled.
>- ***Appropriation price:*** this is the price at which the company creates a unit. It is calculated on an **offer basis**, i.e. using the market's "offer price" for the underlying assets plus purchases expenses, taxes and accrued income.
>	- This represents the amount of money of money that must be put into the fund for the creation of a unit
>- ***Expropriation price:*** this is the price at which the company cancels a unit. It is calculated on a **bid basis**, i.e. using the market's "bid price" (the proceeds from selling the assets minus sale expenses)

> [!money-tree] Unit pricing
>1. The appropriation price is the price at which a unit would be created determined as:
>	- the net asset value of the fund on an offer basis
>	- divided by the number of existing units at the time of the calculation
>2. The net asset value of the fund on an offer basis is calculated as:
>	1. The **market value of the assets** obtained from the exchange (mark-to-market)
>	2.  If some assets do not have an **observable market value**, e.g. property a **professional valuation** may be needed
>	3. Alternatively, a **mark-to-model** approach may be used. A model may project expected cashflows using financial economic assumptions  and the cashflows are discounted.
>	4. Any **expenses involved in purchasing** additional assets must be added (incl. stamp duty)
>	5. **Current assets** must be added (e.g. cash on deposit)
>	6. **Current liabilities** must be deducted (e.g. investments purchased but not yet settled)
>	7. **Accrued income** must be added (e.g. interest income on deposits) net of any outgo (e.g. charges)
>	8. **Accrued tax** must be deducted
>	


> [!cycle] Fund pricing bases 
> The company must decide which pricing bases to use depending on the net cashflow of the fund.
> - ***Offer basis***: for an expanding fund where the insurer is a net creator of units
> - ***Bid basis***: for a contracting fund where the insurer is a net canceller of units
>
>In practice, companies do not switch the basis daily based on minor fluctuations, instead, they use a **broad equity** approach where the basis only changes if there is a significant cashflow movement against the current basis.
>

> [!summary] Pricing for policyholder
> Within either the offer or bid basis, the insurer quotes two prices to the policyholder.
> - ***Offer price***: the price at which the policyholder buys units from the company
> - ***Bid price***: the price at which the company buys units back from the policyholder.
>
>The difference between these two is the **bid-offer spread**, which serves as an initial charge to meet management expenses, commissions and profit. The final quoted prices are often **rounded up**, which can be done in favour of either the company or the customer depending on company practice and regulation.

> [!money-tree] Management box
> Some insurers maintain a **management box**, which is an excess of units owned by the company. This allows the company to manage day-to-day unit transactions without constantly buying or selling the underlying assets, which helps to minimize volatility. However the management box exposes the company to investment risk.

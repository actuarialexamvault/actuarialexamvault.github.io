
> [!in-flow] Effect on cashflows
> Actuarial funding fundamentally alters the emergence of profit.
>- ***Figure 12.1***
>![[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Diagrams/Pasted image 20260206084611.png]]
>- ***At inception***: It creates an immediate **positive cashflow** to the insurer's non-unit fund equal to the difference between the premium paid and the actuarially funded reserve.
> - ***Subsequent years***: Regular net cashflows to the insurer's non-unit fund are **reduced** because the fund management charge is based on a smaller reserve and the non-unit fund must pay to buy the missing units. 

>[!skull] Death strain
> - ***Figure 12.2***
> ![[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Diagrams/Pasted image 20260206085404.png]]
>- ***On death***: the insurer's non-unit fund faces an additional liability to pay the difference between the units actually held and the full guaranteed minimum sum assured. This expected additional death cost is a charge on the non-unit fund at each year end.

>[!trending-up] Unit fund growth
>
>- ***Figure 12.3***
> ![[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Diagrams/Pasted image 20260206090332.png]]
> - ***Reserve impact***: The standard reserve will start of higher than the actuarially funded reserve and will be increasing with investment returns. While the actuarially funded approach will increase by **investment returns** and the **buying back of units**. They will both converge as the policy nears maturity.

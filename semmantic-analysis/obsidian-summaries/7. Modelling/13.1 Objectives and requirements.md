
> [!info] What are the known objectives for building a model?
> Actuarial models provide the actuary with a tool to give **appropriate advice** to ensure the company is run on a **sound financial basis**. Models can be used for:
>1. Financial projections
>2. Budgeting and financial planning
>3. Calculating provisions for claims
>4. Proving solvency to the regulator
>5. Pricing new/existing products
>6. Setting actuarial assumptions
>7. Calculating capital requirements
>8. Business strategy
>9. Valuing guarantees/options
>10. Assessing reinsurance requirements

**VARIABLE CRISP DATA** 

---

### **VARIABLE**

- **V**alid: The model must be valid and produce **realistic and useful results** across a wide range of circumstances.
    
- **A**dequately documented: Comprehensive documentation is essential so users understand the model's **workings, assumptions, and limitations**.
    
- **R**igorous: Must be sufficiently rigorous to ensure the mathematical foundations are sound and results are reliable.
    
- **I**nputs: Assumptions and parameter values must be appropriate for the specific company and the current **economic and business environment**.
    
- **A**rbitrage free: (Implicit in _Sound Inputs/Joint Behavior_) Ensures economically equivalent cashflows are priced consistently.
    
- **B**ehaviour reasonable: The model should exhibit **sensible joint behavior** (consistency) between variables, such as inflation and interest rates.
    
- **L**ength/expense: The model should not be **too long or expensive to run**; it must remain practical for its specific purpose.
    
- **E**asy to understand: The model’s logic should be **easy to appreciate** for both the developer and the user.
    

---

### **CRISP**

- **C**ommunicable & **C**lear: Outputs must be **displayed clearly** for those receiving the advice, and the workings must be transparent.
    
- **R**isk profile: It must include all features of the business that significantly affect advice, such as **mortality, expenses, and investment returns**.
    
- **I**ndependent verification: Model outputs (like projected revenue accounts) must be capable of being **independently verified** for reasonableness.
    
- **S**ensible joint behaviour & **S**imple: Variables must interact logically, and the model should not be **overly complex** unless the detail is strictly required.
    
- **P**arameters: Must include all significant features of the business that affect the final advice being given.
    

---

### **DATA**

- **D**evelopable & **R**efineable: The model should be designed to allow for **subsequent refinement** as new data or needs emerge.
    
- **A**pplications: The model should be robust enough to handle various circumstances and potential use-cases.
    
- **T**ransparency: Linked to documentation; the "black box" element should be minimized so logic is accessible.
    
- **A**ppropriate Results: Results must be **realistic** and fit for the specific purpose for which the model was built.

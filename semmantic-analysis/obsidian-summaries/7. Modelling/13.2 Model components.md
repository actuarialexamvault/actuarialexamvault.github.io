
> [!EXAMPLE] Model components
> To satisfy these requirements, the components of the model must allow for all those features of the business being modelled that could **significantly affect the advice being given**:
> - ***Model points***: These are data records representing **homogeneous groups of policies** chosen to reflect the **distribution of the business** without needing to model every individual policy.
> - **Parameters**: A parameter must be included in the model if the decision reached would alter for different values of that parameter e.g. interest rates. 
> - ***Time period (frequency)*** of cashflow projection must be short enough to produce reliable results while being cost-effective. 
> - ***Future Cashflows***: inclusion of all material cashflows, including premiums, investment income, benefit payments, expenses that are relevant to the business being modelled.
> - ***Dynamic correlations***: The ability to model interactions between assets and liabilities, such as bonus declarations moving in line with investment performance.
> - ***Reserving cashflows***: Allowance for the notional cashflows arising from setting up **supervisory reserves and required solvency margins**. The investment income from the supervisory reserve should also be included as a positive contribution to the profit flow.
> - ***Stochastic capability***: For products with financial guarantees or options, the model may need to use stochastic simulation (Monte Carlo methods) rather than deterministic approaches.
> 

> [!in-flow] Profit calculation
> The total profit arising over the year is the **change in free assets** and can be calculated as: $$Profit= (A_{t+1} - V_{t+1}) - (A_t - V_t) = (A_{t+1​}−A_t​)−(V_{t+1​}−V_t​)$$
> Where:
>- $(A_{t+1}​−A_t​)$ represents the change in total assets, which includes **all physical investment income** earned on the assets backing the reserves.
>- $(V_{t+1}​−V_t​)$ represents the notional increase in the reserve liability.

> [!out-flow] Accounting vs Actuarial context
> |Accounting |Actuarial|Context |
|---|---|---|
|**Assets (**A**)**|All real assets owned (Free assets plus reserve)|Physical resources used to meet obligations.|
|**Liabilities (**L**)**|Requires Reserves $(V_t)$|The company's "promise" to the policyholder.|
|**Equity (**E**)**|Free assets / Surplus / Capital|The cushion that protects the company's solvency. What belongs to shareholders or with-profits policyholders|

>[!info] Distinction between real and notional cashflows
>A supervisory reserve is a liability figure on a balance sheet, but it represents the amount physical assets that a company is legally required to set aside to meet future obligations. 
>- The increase in reserves is labelled "notional" because no money physically leaves the company, it is simply moved on the balance sheet from a category of free assets to required assets.
>- Real contribution: because these assets are physically held by the company, they earn real investment income
>The investment income on the reserve ($V$) is a "real" positive contribution because it increases the total pool of assets ($A$) available to the company

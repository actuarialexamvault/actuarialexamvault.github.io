
> [!solvency] Traditional (passive) approach
> This method assumes a risk-free rate of return (such as on government bonds) and then adds a **margin** for the riskier assets like equities. 
> 
> Assets may be valued using historic cost or book value, which is insensitive to short-term market changes.
> 
> Assumptions are typically derived from **historical experience** and industry data, often with added **prudential margins** to ensure solvency

> [!trajectories] The financial economic (market-consistent) approach
>The financial economic approach assumes **market-consistent values** for parameters.
>
>The assumed investment return should be the **risk-free rate** (regardless of asset type) as the potential additional returns from more risky assets should be exactly cancelled out by their increased risk.
>
>**Assets are valued at market value**. 
>Market-consistent values of the liabilities can be calculated as the **current market values of the (risk-free) assets that match the liability** cashflows.
>
>Assessing the market value of other (non-financial) aspects of the liability (like mortality) is more problematic. There may be some actual market valuations of liabilities available – e.g. for traded endowments.

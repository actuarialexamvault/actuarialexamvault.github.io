
> [!info] Risk discount rate 
> The most common way to allow for the statistical risk associated with parameter values (assumptions) in cashflow modelling is by using a **risk discount rate** that includes a **risk premium** over the risk-free rate of return. 
>
>
> - ***Prudential margins in assumptions***: such as using higher-expected mortality rates for life insurance products or lower than expected interest rates for savings products
> - **Stochastic modelling**: assigning probability distributions to unknown parameters rather than a single value.
> 	- the main difficulty is what probability distribution to assign and how to parameterize it, sensitivity analysis is a more pragmatic and transparent way to handle parameter risk without the **risk of spurious accuracy** caused by deciding upon some arbitrary probability distribution. 

>[!tied] Sensitivity analysis
>- ***Deterministic sensitivity analysis***: involves varying one or more parameter values at a time to observe the financial impact on profit or solvency. 
>	- **Identifying Vulnerabilities:** It helps the actuary understand which factors the product’s profitability is **most sensitive to** (e.g., if a 10% increase in lapses wipes out all profit).
>	- **Scenario Testing:** Actuaries can also test "scenarios" where several correlated variables are adjusted together, such as a high-inflation scenario combined with high interest rates

>[!trending-down] Market consistent approach
>Liabilities are valued to be consistent with current market prices of assets that would replicate the liabilities cashflows, this approaches uses a combination of risk-free discount rates and the addition of explicit risk margins.
>	- **risk-free discountin**g: future cashflows are discounted at the risk free rate 
>	- **risk neutral asset valuation**: the expected investment returns are set to the risk-free rate regardless of the actual assets held, because any potential higher yield from risky assets is viewed as being offset by the increased risk of not achieving that yield. 
>	- **risk margins**: because some risks (like mortality) are not traded, a risk margin is added to the best estimate.


> [!receipt] Profitability
> While the product is required to be profitable as whole, profitability is typically assessed across three dimensions:
> - ***Savings***: the company aims to make a profit on investment income by setting interest rate assumptions that are prudent relative to the expected investment environment.
> 	- Charges must be sufficient to cover initial expenses, recurring expenses and portfolio set-up costs
> 	- Profitability should be assessed against the company's required return or profit criteria
> 	- Profitability will be sensitive to volumes, lapse rates, fund growth, expense overruns and the level of charges
> - ***Protection***: the pricing basis for mortality or morbidity must adequately cover the risks involved including expected future trends in experience.
> - ***Administration***: expense loadings must be set to cover marginal acquisition and administration costs, as well as providing a contribution toward the company's fixed overheads.

> [!caution] Constraints on profitability
>Designers must also consider the impact on profitability of:
>- ***Competition***: insurers may lower the per-policy profit contribution to increase volumes or remain competitive in a market.  
>- ***Cross-subsidies***: large policies may subsize the **fixed costs** of small policies to remain competitive across all sum assured bands.
>- ***Regulatory requirements***: regulatory **caps** on charges, legal requirement to **treat customers fairly** and meet **policyholders reasonable expectation**s regarding profit distribution. 

> [!perc] Profit criteria
> ***NPV***: discounted value of the profit signature using a risk discount rate, often expressed as a percentage of initial commission or the present value of future premiums
> ***IRR***: The discount rate at which the net present vales of cashflows equals zero
> ***DPP***: the time required for the accumulated profits discounted at the risk discount rate) to equal the initial investment

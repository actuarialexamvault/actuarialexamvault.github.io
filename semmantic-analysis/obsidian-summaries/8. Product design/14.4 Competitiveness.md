
> [!example] Factors influencing competitiveness
>
>- ***Distribution channels***: The degree of price sensitivity depends **heavily** on the intended [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/4. External environment/10.2 Distribution channels|method of sale]]:
> - **Contract type**: Different products compete on different elements of their pricing basis
> 	- *Term assurance*:  mortality and fixed expense loadings
> 	- *Single premium endowments*: interest rate assumption and expense loadings
> 	- *Savings products*: past investment performance, charges and brand reputation
> 	- *Group life/pensions*: customer service and administration
> 
> To avoid the race on price only, the company may seek **product differentiation** through
>- Innovation: unique design features, options and guarantees
>- Complexity: e.g. tiered benefits, bundled products
>

> [!math] The profit-volume relationship
> The primary objective is to **maximize total contributions** to the firms fixed overheads and profit from the product as a *whole* not per single policy.  $$[total \ contribution] = [per \ policy \ contribution] \times [number \ of \ policies \ sold]$$
>
>Where the per-policy contribution is the **profit from a single policy net of marginal costs** (ignoring overhead costs). 
>
>The company's total profit is then:
>$$[Total \ profit] = \left\{ \sum\limits_{all \ products} [total \ contributions] \right\} - [overheads] $$
>
>In highly competitive markets, an insurer may choose a design with lower per-policy contributions to ensure high sales volume, thereby maximizing the total contribution.  

>[!caution] Constraints on competitiveness
>
>- **Cross-Subsidies:** A simple, competitive premium structure may require large policies to **subsidize the fixed administrative costs** of small policies to ensure the smaller policies do not appear unreasonably expensive to the public.
>
>- **Regulation:** insurers are in competition with non-insurance companies (banks, unit-trusts and investment trust) for savings business, which are subject to **different regulatory** controls which may create a **non-level playing field**.

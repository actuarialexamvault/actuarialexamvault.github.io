> [!solvency] Capital management 
> The capital management strategy and availability of free assets will heavility influence product design:
>- ***Recouping capital***: the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Capital requirements#^d8b18a|company's objective]] in capital management will heavily influence product design. 
>- ***Capital constraints***: if a company has limited [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Free Assets|free assets]], it must prioritize designs that minimize the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/New Business Strain#^46c892|initial capital requirement]] to ensure it remains solvent while meeting its sales goals.
>- ***Opportunity cost***: choosing to write capital-intensive business represents an opportunity costs, as that capital is no longer available to fund other investment/growth ventures.

> [!important] How different product types and designs influence financing requirements
>- ***Unit-Linked vs. Non-Linked***: unit-linked products have significantly less capital requirements than non-linked products because they pass the investment risk to the policyholder and often have reviewable charges for mortality and expenses, leading to lower supervisory reserves.
>- ***Guarantees***: the inclusion of [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/onerous|onerous]] guarantees (such as minimum maturity benefits or guaranteed annuity rates) increases the capital requirement because the insurer must hold additional reserves to cover the possibility of those guarantees "biting".
>- ***Charging structures***: charging structures that allow the company to recoup its initial expenses early are more capital efficient.
>- ***Premium frequency***: single premium contracts are more capital efficient than regular monthly premiums.

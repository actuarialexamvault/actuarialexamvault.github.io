
> [!caution] Risks to the insurer
>Offering guarantees creates two significant risks for the insurer:
>
>- ***Increased capital strain***: supervisory regulations require companies to set up higher reserves to cover the possibility of a guarantee being exercised. This ties up more capital, increasing [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/New Business Strain|new business strain]].
>- ***Unexpected costs***: While supervisory regulations require **prudent assumption**s to ensure a high probability that liabilities will be met, if actual market conditions deviate from the assumed conditions the cost of the guarantee biting may be in excess of the reserve.

> [!info] Factors affecting the onerousness of guarantees
>The onerousness of any guarantee is determined by factors that influence both the **potential for unexpected costs** and the **amount of capital** the insurer must set aside from the outset.
>- Size of the guarantee 
>- Duration
>- Significance of reserves: the impact depends on the nature of the product, for instance a minimum investment yield guarantee is a much greater risk on a savings-heavy product than on a low reserve product like term assurance. 
>- Volume of business sold with the guarantee
>- Available capital to absorb the initial reserving cost and potential pay outs
>- The likelihood and impact of adverse changes
>- Management actions
> 
>

> [!trajectories] Sensitivity of profit
> Products are more likely to be sensitive to particular types of risks the more onerous the guarantees are. The components that affect profitability are:
> - [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/5. Risks/11.3 Investment risk|investment return]]
> - [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/5. Risks/11.2 Mortality and morbidity risk|mortality, or other contingency if relevant]]
> - [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/5. Risks/11.4 Expense risk|expenses, including expense inflation]]
> - [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/5. Risks/11.5 Withdrawal risk|withdrawal rates]]
> 

> [!example] Product designs that reduce the sensitivity of profit to adverse experience
> The product design of a unit-linked contract can minimise the sensitivity of profitability by:
> - Investment return: if there are no investment guarantees, then most of the risk is borne by the policyholder
> - Mortality: reviewable charges at the company's discretion
> - Withdrawal rates: don't offer any guaranteed surrender values
> - Matching: try to match income (the charges) with outgo (expenses and benefit costs) as closely as possible by duration, especially with regard to initial expenses.
> 
>It may be possible to redesign non-linked contracts to **reduce the sensitivity of profit to adverse variations in future experience**  (e.g. by not offering any guaranteed surrender values, using commission systems with **[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/clawback clause|clawback]] for early withdrawals**). 
>
>Non-linked **with-profits contracts** could be redesigned to reduce the guarantees associated with the **bonus distribution method**.

>[!definition] Cross-subsidy definition
>[[Cross-subsidy]] refers to the sharing of non-risk elements, primarily expenses, where certain groups of policyholders contribute more than their fair share to support others. It is distinct from **mutuality**, which focuses on the **pooling and sharing of insurance risks** like mortality or investment volatility.

>[!explainer] Mutuality vs. Cross-Subsidy
>**Mutuality** involves the pooling and sharing of insurance risks (e.g., mortality, investment volatility) across policyholders. **Cross-subsidy** involves the sharing of non-risk elements — primarily expenses — where some policyholders pay more than their fair share to subsidize others. Cross-subsidy is a product design choice, while mutuality is a fundamental insurance principle.

> [!receipt] Cross-subsidy by policy/premium size
> Insurers incur fixed per-policy costs that remain constant regardless of the size of the policy.
> ***Conflict***: charging the full cost to small policies may make them uncompetitive or appear expensive to consumers.
> ***Design solution***: To ensure marketability, an actuary may design a product with a reduced fixed policy fee, recouping the shortfall by increasing the charges expressed as a percentage of the premium.
> ***The result***: this creates a cross-subsidy where larger policies overpay to subsidize the administration of smaller policies.
> This may be viewed as unfair by policyholders that have large policies/premiums.

> [!trajectories] Trade-off between simplicity and equity
> A major factor in product design is to manage the trade off between a simple, marketable charging structure and the desire to avoid cross-subsidies.
> Simple structures often create **unintentional cross-subsidies** across different ages, terms, or policy sizes.
> Any **deliberate design** that chooses not to reflect **known differences in risk** in the premium for **marketing or simplicity** reasons is seen as a cross-subsidy.

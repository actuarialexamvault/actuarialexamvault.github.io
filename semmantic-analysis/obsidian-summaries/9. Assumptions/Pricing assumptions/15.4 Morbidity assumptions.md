
> [!cycle] Income protection (IP)
> IP benefits are modelled using a **multi-state model**, tracking policyholders through states like healthy, sick (within deferred period), claiming, recovered or dead. 
> 
> Actuaries must **estimate transition intensities** (inception, recovery, and death rates), which should be subdivided by homogeneous groups such as age, duration of claim and type of disability: 
> - ***Duration of claims***: recovery rates **reduce significantly as the duration of the claim increases**. A policyholder who has been sick for a week is much more likely to recover soon than one who has been incapacitated for a year.
> - ***Type of disability***: recovery rates differ significantly according to the underlying cause of the sickness or disability. For example, the path to recovery for a broken limb is very different from that of a chronic mental illness.
> - ***Claims history***: A sophisticated model may subdivide the healthy state based on whether a policyholder has claimed previously because **individuals who have had periods off work due to sickness in the past are statistically more likely to claim again in the future**
> 
> These intensities are heavily influenced by **[[../../../Definitions/Economic morale|Economic morale]]**, the provision of government welfare, [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/2. Health and care products/5.1.2 Income Protection (IP) contracts - Features#^2c5f8d|rehabilitation benefits]], [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/4. External environment/10.6 The tax regime#^782f7b|tax]] and the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/replacement ratio|replacement ratio]].
> Once transition intensities have been estimated, the are converted into **transition probabilities**, which are used to calculate **expected numbers or proportions of lives** in each each state at future ages. 

> [!caution] Critical illness
> Setting CI assumptions requires estimating frequency distributions for up to $40^+$ medical conditions.  
> These assumptions are highly sensitive to **medical advances**, which can impact 
> - **cure rates** (curability leads to **[[../../../Definitions/Windfall claims|windfall claims]]** / **prevention** leads to reduced claims)
> - **earlier diagnosis** (increasing claim rates), or make 
> - **surgical procedures** become more available. 
>
>Medical advances will have a different impact on **[[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/Why medical advances will have a different impact on new vs existing business in critical illness insurance|new vs existing business]]**.
> 
> Modelling and assumptions is further complicated by:
> - use of **disease-based** and **treatment-based** definitions of claims, which may [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/Why disease-based and treatment-based claims need to be modelled separately in CI|need to be modelled separately]]
> - **guaranteed and reviewable** alternatives
> - **data limitations**

> [!family] LTCI
> For LTCI actuaries must estimate both the ***distribution of claim frequency*** and the ***distribution of claim amount*** (for [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/2. Health and care products/7.1.2 Long-Term Care Insurance - Product variations#^e87488|indemnity products]]). 
> The distribution of claim amounts is sensitive to the ***economy***, ***inflation***, and the ***supply of long-term care services relative to demand***.
> Other influences on claims distributions include ***advances in medicine***, ***inflation of care costs*** and ***data limitations***.

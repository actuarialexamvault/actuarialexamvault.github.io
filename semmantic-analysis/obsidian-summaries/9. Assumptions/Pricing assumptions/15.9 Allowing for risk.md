 
> [!list] Methods of allowing for margins
> - risk premium in the discount rate
> - stochastic modelling 
> - explicit margins to individual parameter values

> [!in-flow] Risk discount rate
> The RDR method allows for risk by increasing the rate used to discount the profit signature, requiring a higher hurdle for profitability. 
>1. **Baseline**:  Start with a risk-free rate of return, typically from government bond yields
>2. **Add a risk premium**: use [[UNISA/DSC4825 - Mathematical finance 1/Summaries/3.6 Capital Pricing Model (CAPM)|CAPM]] to identify the systematic market risk of the company's shares and a corresponding premium to the risk-free rate
>3. **Adjust for product-specific risk**: increase the premium further for riskier product features such as high guarantees, policyholder options or a lack of historical data
>4. **Assess statistical risk**: ensure the chosen RDR is sufficient to cover the **variance** of the product's expected return. 
>	1. Analytically using the variances of the parameter values used
>	2. Using sensitivity analyses with deterministically assessed variations in the parameter values
>	3. By using stochastic models for some, or all, of the parameter values and simulations
>	4. By comparison with any available market rate

> [!trajectories] Stochastic approach
> This method uses probability distributions and simulations to quantify risk and determine appropriate safety levels
> 1. ***Assign probability distributions*** to unknown future parameters (like investment returns) e.g. probability density functions.  
> 2. ***Generate simulations***: Use [[Monte Carlo]] simulation to run thousands of "sample paths" (e.g. 5k to 10k) for the parameters.
> 3. ***Project cashflows***: for each individual simulation, calculate the resulting profit or shortfall based on the generated parameter values
> 4. ***Determine the margin***: Analyse the results to find a specific percentile (such as the 99.5% confidence level) that ensures the company can meet its obligations under all scenarios.

> [!out-flow] Explicit margins
> Explicit margins involve adjusting individual best estimate assumptions directly to produce a prudent result.
> 1. ***Derive best estimates***: determine the expected value for each parameter based on historical experience and anticipated future conditions
> 2. ***Evaluate significance***: Assess the financial significant and degree of risk associated with each parameter using sensitivity analysis
> 3. ***Apply adjustments***: direct adjustments are made to each parameter, for example subtracting a margin from the best estimate investment return or adding a margin to expected mortality rates
> 4. ***Ensure consistency***: reflect realistic long-term relationships between variables like inflation and interest rates.

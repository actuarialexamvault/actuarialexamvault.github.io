
> [!out-flow] Valuation basis
> The basis of a valuation should reflect:
> - expected future experience
> - margins to ensure adequacy of reserves
> - legislation/regulation (for published reserves)
> - the need for consistency
> The basis of a valuation will depend on whether the accounts are to be published or are only for internal use.
> 

> [!abstract] Liabilities
> $$ \text{Liabilities} = \text{PV of Future Claims} + \text{PV of Expenses} - \text{PV of Premiums} $$
> 

> [!solvency] Supervisory reserves
> The main objective of a supervisory valuation is to provide a **minimum standard of security for policyholders**.
> - Meeting obligations
> - Compliance
> - Public confidence

> [!receipt] Published accounts
> Determining liabilities to be shown in public financial reports, :
> - which may be prepared on either a **going concern or break-up basis**
> - required to show a true and fair view
> - reserves may be required to assessed on **best estimate basis or some other basis**

> [!steps] Internal management accounts
> Valuations conducted for internal purposes give a **realistic view** of the company's financial health, undistorted by regulatory prudence.
> - Measuring realistic profitability
> - Determining embedded value (EV)
> - Profit distribution (bonuses)
> - Performance management (KPIs)
>

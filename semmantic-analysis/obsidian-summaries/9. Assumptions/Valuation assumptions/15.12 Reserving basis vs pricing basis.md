
> [!trajectories] Uncertainty - pricing vs reserving
> The level of uncertainty is generally **lower for reserving than for pricing** because the **policies are already in force**.
> - ***Demographic assumptions***: uncertainty is reduced because the actuary knows who the policyholders are and can analyse the **historical experience** of that specific class of lives.
> - ***Expense assumptions***: are also easier to assess in reserving because initial acquisition expenses have already been paid and the volume of existing business is more predictable. Pricing expense assumptions are more uncertain as they rely on **expected future new business volumes** and the expected **mix of contracts sold**.
> Pricing often involves new or innovative products or untested markets where historical data is lacking, whereas reserving deals with an established portfolio with know risk.

>[!solvency] Prudency - pricing vs reserving
>In general the statutory valuation basis is likely to be more prudent than the pricing basis:
>- ***Mortality***: Valuation basis is likely to have higher mortality rates
>- ***Investment return***: Valuation basis is likely to use lower returns
>- ***Expenses***: Valuation basis likely to assume higher expenses and higher expense inflation
>- ***Variable expenses***: Variable expenses, .e.g commission are likely to be the same in both bases
>- ***Tax*** is likely to be the same in both bases
>- ***Real yield***: The gap between investment return and inflation will be lower in the valuation basis
>- ***Lapses***: need to be more conservative for reserving, but depending on various factors a higher or lower assumption could be more prudent
>
>Items not part of the valuation basis:
>- initial expenses
>- initial commission
>- expenses volume and mix of new business 

> [!info] Using pricing assumptions to calculate reserves 
> ***Prudent assumptions***: it is standard practice in certain jurisdictions use assumptions with [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/9. Assumptions/Pricing assumptions/15.9 Allowing for risk#^30e405|explicit margins]] for prudency in pricing and then use those same assumptions to calculate supervisory reserves. This approach is most suitable for **with-profit contracts** because they are priced with large margins, allowing surplus to emerge and be return as bonuses if actual experience is better than assumed. The approach is less justifiable in the case of without-profit contracts because these are priced with small margins to remain competitive and may not meet the supervisory requirement for prudence.
> ***Using RDR for reserving***: it is inappropriate to use pricing assumptions for reserving when premiums are based on best-estimate assumptions with risk allowed for through a [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/9. Assumptions/Pricing assumptions/15.9 Allowing for risk#^8b2558|risk discount rate (RDR)]]

> [!explainer] [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/Question 22.2 - Why you can't use RDR and BE assumptions for reserving]]

> [!sign]  Sign-of the reserve
>- For a positive reserve (PV outgo > PV income), decreasing the discount rate,  increases PV liabilities which increases prudence
>- For a negative reserve (PV outgo < PV income), a higher discount rate, decreases PV of future profit, which increases prudence (avoids taking excessive credit for future profits)


> [!definition] 
> These reserves represent a **realistic view** of the company's financial ability to meet its obligations. They are primarily used in **internal management accounts**.

> [!sum] Calculating best estimate reserves
> - ***Assumptions***: The actuary uses best-estimate assumptions for all variables, including mortality, investment return, expenses and persistency. These assumptions are derived from historical experience adjusted for expected future trends (without adding any margins).
> - ***Cashflow method***: A discounted cashflow approach is typically used to estimate the amounts needed today to meet future claims and expenses, offset by expected premium and investment income. 
> - **No elimination of negative reserves**: Unlike prudent supervisory valuations, best estimate valuations allow for negative reserves, which treat future expected profits as an asset rather than setting them to zero.

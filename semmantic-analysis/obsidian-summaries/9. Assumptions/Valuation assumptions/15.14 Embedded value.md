
> [!question] What is embedded value?
> Embedded value is the **present value of future shareholder profit** in respect of the existing business of a company, including the **shareholder owned net assets**.
> The purpose of an embedded value is to give a realistic assessment of the **value of the insurance company** to its shareholders.
> It is also used as a starting point in calculating the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Appraisal Value|Appraisal Value]] of a life insurance company.

^00d738

>[!math] How is embedded value calculated?
>Embedded value is calculated as the sum of:
>- Shareholders share of excess assets over reserves
>	- Recorded at market value or discounted at a lower discount rate to reflect "lock-in" because they are invested cautiously
>- The present value of future shareholder profits arising on existing business (PVFP). 
>	- Discount of the [[../../../Definitions/Profit signature|Profit signature]] for existing business, the calculation may differ by type of business:
>	- *Conventional without-profit =* $\text{PV (Net cashflow + Interest on reserves - Change in reserves)}$
>	- *Unit-linked*: $\text{PV (Non-unit cashflow + Interest on non-unit reserves - Change in non-unit reserves)}$
>	- *With-profit*: $\text{PV (Future shareholder transfers)}$

> [!explainer] [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Explainers/Explain why for without-profit business, embedded value is effectively the release of margins held within supervisory reserves relative to the assumptions used within the embedded value calculation|Explain why for without-profit business, embedded value is effectively the release of margins held within supervisory reserves relative to the assumptions used within the embedded value calculation]]

> [!info] How to determine assumptions (EV Basis)?
> To calculate EV we need two bases: 
> - ***Reserving basis***: use to calculate net assets and future changes in reserves to get the profit to shareholders
> - ***Projection basis***: projects actual best estimate experience to estimate future shareholder profits
>
>The nature of the projection assumptions depends on why the EV is being calculated:
>- ***Internal management or published accounts***: use best estimate assumptions
>- ***Sale of a company***: The seller uses best estimate assumptions to show the highest justifiable [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Definitions/Appraisal Value|appraisal value]]. 
>- ***Purchase of a company***: A buyer might use cautious assumptions that include margins for risk to avoid overpaying.

> [!trending-down] How to allow for risk when calculating embedded values
> There are two primary methods to account for unpredictability of future profits. 
> - ***Risk discount rate(RDR)***: The more risky the profit stream the higher the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/9. Assumptions/Pricing assumptions/15.9 Allowing for risk#^8b2558|risk discount rate]] 
> - ***Market-consistent approach***: uses risk-free rate for discount liabilities and assumes investment earn risk-free rate. A separate risk margin (using the [[../../10. Reserving/9. Cost of capital approach|cost of capital approach]]) is then explicitly deducted from the total.
> - ***Stochastic approach***: A stochastic projection would highlight the range of possible values that future profit might take.
> 

> [!abstract] What is the difference between a **Best estimate** vs **Embedded value** valuation?
> - ***Best estimate valuation***: calculates the present value liability using realistic assumptions and compares the total to the current assets to provide a measure of realistic solvency: $\text{(Value of assets) - (Realistic Liability/Reserve)}$
> - ***Embedded value***: focuses on shareholder profit, by adding the shareholder's share of net assets to the **present value of future profits that emerge in excess of reserves.** 
> 	- $\text{(Net Assets) + (Present value of future shareholder profits)}$
> 	- Where: $\text{(Net Assets) = (Value of assets) - (Supervisory reserve)}$
> 
> The EV calculation will give a slightly different result than a realistic profit calculation because it allows for the shareholders cost of capital. The difference stems from the deferral of profits.
> - Life companies are required to hold supervisory reserves, which are prudent/larger than realistic reserves.
> - Profits cannot be distributed to shareholders until they arise in excess of these prudent supervisory reserves. This locks in capital within the company.
> - EV discounts these **future profits** using the [[ASSA/F102 Life Insurance Principles/Summary/9. Assumptions/Pricing assumptions/15.9 Allowing for risk#^|risk discount rate (RDR)]] which is typically higher than the realistic investment return earned on the reserves.
> - Under **market consistent valuations** the EV calculation deducts an explicit risk margin to account for the opportunity cost to shareholders of having capital locked in by the company.
> 	- $\text{EV = (Assets - BEL) - Risk Margin}$ 
>
>This means that EV produces a lower valuation because it accounts for the **opportunity cost** to shareholders of having their profits deferred while being held as required supervisory reserves.

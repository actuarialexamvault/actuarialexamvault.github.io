
> [!Abstract] The need for consistency
> Assumptions cannot be set in isolation, they must be considered in totality to reflect how variables behave together in the real world.

> [!cycle] Joint behaviour of variables
> - ***Dynamic Correlations***: Models must show the joint behaviour between variables that are not independent, such as **inflation and interest rates**.
> 

>[!definition] Consitency between bases
>- ***Asset-liability valuations*** must be consistent, for example if assets are valued at market value, the investment return assumption for liabilities must reflect current market yields.>
>- ***Pricing vs Reserving***: The reserves assumed during the pricing process must align with the actual supervisory reserving basis. If actual reserves are more prudent than those priced for, the cost of capital will be higher than allowed for in premiums, reducing profitability.
>- ***Embedded value (EV)***: The reserves used to calculate "net assets" must be consistent with the reserves used to determine the present value of future profits. Furthermore, the EV basis should be compared side-by-side with the pricing basis to understand the value of new business.   
>- ***Previous basis***: Any new basis should use the previous basis as a starting point. Any changes must be justifiable and the financial impact of each change should be reported separately.
